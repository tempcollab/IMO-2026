# Proof-reviewer memory (persistent across rounds)

ALWAYS: independently re-derive the single load-bearing identity/lemma with a quick Python script (exact Fraction arithmetic for game values, differential_evolution/brute force for minimax claims) rather than trusting the write-up's algebra — caught real issues cheaply this way (round 1, imo-2026-03).

ALWAYS: when a "dead-end"/impossibility approach chains inequalities to reach a contradiction, check whether every inequality in the chain is independently established, or whether one of them (often disguised as "not in dispute, established by approach X") is actually the very open gap that approach X itself still flags as unsolved. This is a subtle overclaim pattern distinct from ordinary hand-waving: the proof is internally rigorous but its premise silently borrows an unproven external fact (round 1, imo-2026-03: equalization-potential-bound's Lemma D/E argument needed V(p*)=c(n) exactly, which was actually the shared open gap of the other two approaches, not an established fact).

ALWAYS: when multiple approaches converge on proving the same sub-case independently (e.g. two builders both prove a "k=0" trivial sub-case via the same argument), treat this as a genuine positive cross-check, not redundant work — it increases confidence the shared lemma is correctly stated.

NEVER: accept a builder's "Status: unsolved, dead end confirmed" self-report at face value just because the mechanism (e.g. an LP/polytope impossibility lemma) is individually correct — trace every premise the mechanism's chain of inequalities depends on back to what is actually proven elsewhere in the population.

NEVER: let a proof file's own internal messiness (e.g. a "wait, let me recheck" mid-derivation) automatically read as a red flag — verify computationally whether the final claimed result is correct regardless of how clean the prose is; conversely don't let polished prose substitute for verification.

ALWAYS: when a builder proves a "generalized" lemma (e.g. "for any tail shape" not just the geometric config), test it with genuinely random/arbitrary inputs, not just inputs resembling the original special case — this is exactly what distinguishes a real general lemma (Lemma DOM, Lemma HALVE, round 2, imo-2026-03) from an accidental special-case coincidence, and both approaches in round 2 correctly delivered fully general, verified lemmas this way.

ALWAYS: treat a builder's explicit refutation of a natural "shortcut" lemma (with an exact-fraction counterexample) as a first-class, certifiable negative result on par with a positive lemma — it prunes the search space for every other approach and should be written into its own lemmas/ file so future rounds don't re-attempt the same shortcut (round 2, imo-2026-03: merge-by-sums-alone counterexample).

NEVER: penalize an approach's verdict for citing supporting numerical diagnosis (e.g. "scipy found the true optimum splits two non-adjacent pieces") that is explicitly not used as a load-bearing proof step — distinguish decoration/motivation from actual proof content before flagging a gap.

ALWAYS: when a "dead-end" approach's build was explicitly told by a prior round's gate to STOP after one specific check (e.g. "reproduce the falsification, then stop, do not proceed to later steps"), verify the build actually stopped there and treat that as correct compliance, not a shortfall — don't ding an approach for "not attempting Step 2/3/4" when the mandated gate told it not to (round 4→5 catch-up, imo-2026-03: majorization-smoothing correctly stopped after Step 0).

ALWAYS: when an approach proves an abstract/generalized lemma (scalar-summary reduction, e.g. "Claim ★" reducing a merge inequality to just max(T) and oddrank(T)) AND separately proves it FALSE for a wider parameter range (e.g. s≥3) with an exact counterexample, certify BOTH the positive lemma and the negative counterexample together in one lemma file — the negative half is exactly as valuable as the positive half since it prunes the search space for every sibling approach (round 4→5, imo-2026-03: Claim ★ s≤2 proved / s≥3 refuted).

ALWAYS: when reviewing a "verified numerically for n=1..k" claim that feeds into an unproven general lemma, write your OWN independent brute-force enumerator from scratch rather than re-running the builder's script — this caught nothing wrong here but is cheap insurance against a builder's script silently encoding the thing it's trying to prove (round 4→5, imo-2026-03: independently re-enumerated Lemma L for n=1..8, matched exactly).

NEVER: assume a builder's "exhaustive enumeration of all K compositions" count is exact without recounting — one approach said "7 compositions" for a 3-piece, budget-2 split when the true count is 10 (itertools.product filtered by sum≤2); this was a harmless descriptive slip, not load-bearing, but always recount claimed case totals since a wrong count can hide a skipped case (round 4→5, imo-2026-03).

ALWAYS: when a builder claims to have "fully proved" a lemma that a prior round only verified numerically, don't just re-run their numeric check at higher n — independently re-derive the *mechanism itself* (e.g. re-implement the case-split recursion `D=D'` vs `D=t1-D'` from scratch and cross-check it against direct recomputation, not just the final inequality it's meant to establish) — this catches an induction whose final numeric conclusion happens to be right but whose case-split logic is subtly wrong, which a pure "check the conclusion at n=1..12" test cannot catch (round 5, imo-2026-03: Lemma PARITY-PAIR, verified both the conclusion AND the underlying recursion independently, found zero errors in either).

ALWAYS: distinguish a builder's "structural impossibility proof" (e.g. min of a concave and a genuinely convex piece can never be globally concave — true for ANY refinement of the technique) from a "we tried N natural candidates and all failed" plausibility diagnosis when deciding partial vs RETHINK — only the former justifies RETHINK/dead-end; the latter is real negative progress but the approach isn't proven unsalvageable, so keep it partial with a flagged condition for downgrading next round if the "fix" turns out to just duplicate a sibling approach's casework (round 5, imo-2026-03: potential-averaging-bound kept partial, contrasted explicitly with majorization-smoothing's genuine RETHINK).
ALWAYS: for a "tree-reachability" style lemma (e.g. Lemma TREE-BOUND, round 8, imo-2026-03) that claims to close a gap "unconditionally, more strongly than needed" by dropping a budget/depth constraint, write an independent brute-force enumerator over unconstrained tree shapes (not just the budget-restricted subset) and confirm the minimum matches the claimed bound with zero violations, AND check that dropping the constraining hypothesis (here: odd multiplicity r) genuinely breaks the bound in a control case — this positively confirms the hypothesis is load-bearing rather than vestigial, cheaply ruling out a mis-scoped "stronger than needed" overclaim (round 8, imo-2026-03: verified r odd needed by showing r even at m=1 gives D=0<1).
NEVER: accept a citation to "already established in <toolkit file>'s derivation of the (X) formula" at face value when the named formula doesn't literally appear under that name in the cited file — independently re-derive the fact from first principles instead; if it checks out (as it did here, round 8, imo-2026-03's "(BLOCK)" citation) treat it as a minor citation slip, not a correctness flaw, but always verify rather than trust the citation label.

ALWAYS: when a builder generalizes an "at most one X" induction to "arbitrarily many X, anywhere, including several simultaneous" (round 10, imo-2026-03: Lemma TREE-BOUND-RESIDUAL → TREE-BOUND-MULTICLUSTER), specifically stress-test the *simultaneous-at-one-level* case with an exhaustive one-shot enumeration (every independent slot choosing its option at once) rather than only a randomized-recursive generator — a purely recursive random generator can under-sample the "many things tied/impure at exactly the same top-level pass" case that recursion-based induction proofs sometimes handle differently from the "one deep, rest pure" case; this caught nothing wrong here but is the correct targeted test for exactly the scope the round's new lemma claims to add.

ALWAYS: before accepting that an abstract combinatorial model (e.g. a "forest with impure-cut nodes that terminate into two leaves") fully captures every real strategy in the underlying game, probe the model's own restrictive modeling conventions (e.g. "the impure cut is terminal, the residual is never further split") with an *unconstrained* version of the abstraction first (cheap, may show violations) then, if it does, fall back to directly simulating the REAL, budget-constrained game from scratch (bypassing the abstraction entirely) for a couple of small concrete n via random search over every real strategy — this is the only way to tell whether an apparent "gap" in the abstraction's coverage is a real proof gap or just an artifact of not respecting the true resource budget (round 10, imo-2026-03: found D can go to 0 with unconstrained extra splits of a residual, but the real budget-respecting game for n=2,4 matched c(n) exactly with zero violations in 200k-400k trials, confirming no real gap).

ALWAYS: when independently re-verifying a "min over a finite move-menu with a budget parameter" style recursion (round 12, imo-2026-03's Candidate 5), write a from-scratch rejection-sampling random-instance generator with real care about degenerate cases the constraint implies vacuous or trivial — e.g. "sorted descending + p_1<Σ/2" is literally impossible for m=2 (p_1≥p_2 forces p_1≥Σ/2), so a naive `while True: resample until p_1<Σ/2` loop hangs forever at m=2; this is a bug in the reviewer's own harness, not the proof, but wastes real wall-clock time if not caught immediately via per-trial print/flush diagnostics.
ALWAYS: when a builder's adversarial-gate claim rests on an optimizer (differential_evolution) finding an exact closed-form worst-case margin across several m, re-run the optimizer yourself with a freshly-written script (not the builder's) and check the reported margins match the builder's claimed closed form to high precision at every m tested — this is much stronger corroboration than re-running the builder's own script, and is cheap (round 12, imo-2026-03: independently reproduced margin(m)=1/(2(2^m-1)) for m=4..12 exactly).

CRITICAL: when a "certified" recursion has a `budget` parameter meant to model a hard resource cap (e.g. real marks), ALWAYS check by hand-tracing the actual winning move sequence and counting *real* elementary operations used, not just trusting that the recursion's parameter decrements correctly for every move — a well-foundedness proof (termination) says NOTHING about whether the budget parameter faithfully counts the real resource; round 12/13, imo-2026-03: Lemma WF-C5 correctly proved the round-12 `solve(A,budget)` recursion terminates, but `budget` only counted nested Move-3 uses, silently granting Xiang Yu one uncounted extra real mark per Move-3 use, invalidating round 12's entire "adversarial gate PASS" as evidence (the gate bounded an over-generous surrogate, in the wrong direction for the theorem). ALWAYS re-verify a numeric "gate PASS" against a truly resource-capped model (exhaustive continuous optimizer over every real mark-allocation pattern is a reliable from-scratch cross-check) before trusting it as evidence for a theorem, not just against the recursion's own stated budget parameter.

ALWAYS: when a "restricted move-menu" recursion (that respects the true resource cap) computes a value EXCEEDING a target bound, do not conclude the theorem is refuted — first check with an unrestricted brute-force/continuous optimizer whether the TRUE optimum (over ALL strategies, not just the certified menu) still meets the target; a menu-insufficiency (missing move shape, e.g. non-contiguous subset matching) can make a properly-capped-but-restricted recursion look like a counterexample when the true value in fact meets the bound exactly (round 13, imo-2026-03: witness T=(20,15,12,8), restricted solve2 gave 28>27.5, but true 3-mark optimum via non-contiguous match is exactly 27.5=Σ/2).

ALWAYS: when independently reimplementing a multi-branch recursion that includes a "zero-cost move" (e.g. Move 2 with cost=|S|-1 at an exact-residual boundary |S|=1,r=0), check whether that specific zero-cost branch is a literal no-op (reproduces the identical state with identical remaining budget) — a naive from-scratch reimplementation will hit true infinite recursion here unless the zero-cost sub-case is explicitly excluded (usually because a separate "Move 0"/free-tie move already subsumes it); this is not a flaw in the original proof if its write-up already flags the sub-case as "covered by Move 0 so cost is genuinely >=1 whenever separately invoked" — but always verify by actually hitting the bug yourself, since a write-up's remark about a boundary case can be load-bearing for termination, not decorative (round 14, imo-2026-03: solve2's Move 2 |S|=1,r=0 sub-case).

ALWAYS: when a new approach's build report explicitly diagnoses that "any fix/repair of this specific mechanism reduces to duplicating a sibling approach's still-open content, offering no independent leverage," treat this as meeting the bar for RETHINK (a genuine structural-impossibility finding, true for any refinement) even in the approach's FIRST build round, overriding a builder's self-reported `partial` Status — this is the same convergence-failure pattern as minimax-mixed-duality (rounds 6-8) and case-c-secondary-extremality (round 11) in this run, and does not require waiting "2+ rounds" if the convergence claim is proven (not just observed) in round 1 (round 14, imo-2026-03: case-c-slack-covering's one-level averaging family, provably dominated by a single best member that itself provably fails at the uniform-tail boundary for all m>=4, with the builder's own diagnosis showing any fix collapses into the sibling's SLACK-COVER content).

ALWAYS: when a round's construction recurses into an already-certified sub-theorem via a "loose corollary" bound (e.g. Lemma V3-BOUND built atop the certified m=3 theorem V_3), trace the sub-theorem's case formulas back to their original certified source line-by-line (round 16, imo-2026-03: cross-checked V_3's Case A/B/C formulas against lemmas/ptbi-threshold-reduction.md and the round-9 "m=3 solved in full" narrative) rather than trusting the round's own restatement — cheap, and catches transcription errors that a pure "test the final inequality on witnesses" check can miss if the error happens to cancel on those specific witnesses.

ALWAYS: for a region-split closure (e.g. round 16's Region 1/Region 2/Region 3 partition of m=4 Case C), independently re-classify a large random sample into the regions AS DEFINED (not just check the final min-over-strategies inequality) to confirm the partition is exhaustive and disjoint with the claimed boundary conditions — this is a distinct check from "does the bound hold," and catches boundary/case-counting bugs a pure inequality-sampling check would miss (round 16, imo-2026-03: 300k trials classified into exactly one of 3 regions, zero gaps).
