- Distinct openings:
  1. **Claiming-phase reduction first.** Prove (as a standalone lemma, before touching the marking game) that once the piece-length multiset is fixed, the alternating-claim subgame's unique optimal-play outcome is: sort pieces descending, Liu Bang (mover 1) gets the pieces at odd positions (1st, 3rd, 5th, ...), Xiang Yu gets even positions. This collapses the whole problem to a pure combinatorial-optimization game over interval partitions: LB picks ≤n cut points to maximize (over XY's ≤n-point response) the "odd-position sum" of the resulting sorted piece list. This is the natural top-level target and every other opening builds on it.
  2. **"Shave near a cut" as XY's universal weapon.** Marking a point epsilon away from an existing mark splits a big piece into (piece − eps, eps); in the eps→0 limit this doesn't change value much by itself but it changes the *parity* of which pieces land in odd vs even sorted-position — this is XY's tool for "reshuffling parity" cheaply. Any LB configuration must be robust to this move on every one of its pieces.
  3. **"Bisect the biggest piece" as XY's other weapon.** Splitting LB's largest remaining piece exactly in half changes which piece is "biggest" and can force a big piece to fall to an even (XY) sorted-position. The n=1 optimum is exactly the point where the "shave" and "bisect" strategies give XY equal payoff — suggests the general optimum is always a balance point between these two families of XY replies, and the outline should look for a general minimax equalization argument (LB configuration s.t. every XY reply — shave-type or bisect-type or any mix — is simultaneously no better than the claimed c(n)).
  4. **Self-similar/recursive construction for LB.** The n=2 exact optimum (see below) is obtained by nesting an n=1-flavored construction inside a bigger piece; suggests an inductive construction: c(n) built recursively from c(n−1) by embedding the smaller game inside one interval. This points toward proving the recursion directly (e.g. c(n) in terms of c(n-1)) rather than guessing the closed form outright.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all labeled CONJECTURE / numerically-supported unless stated "proved"):
  - **Claiming-phase lemma (PROVED numerically, should be easy to prove rigorously)**: for a fixed multiset of piece lengths, brute-force minimax game-tree search (exact, via memoized recursion) agrees with the "sort descending, take alternate positions starting with mover 1" rule on 2000 random trials with 1-6 pieces, no mismatches.   - **n=1 (SOLVED exactly by direct analysis + grid verification)**: LB marks one point at x. XY's best replies are of two types: (a) "shave," placing y→x, giving LB→x as y→x (infimum x, approached not attained, but the guarantee is exactly x since the inf is x); (b) "bisect the piece containing x on the correct side," giving LB total 1 − x/2 for x > 1/2 (splits [0,x] into two pieces of x/2 each, competing against remaining piece 1−x). XY's actual best response is min(x, 1−x/2). LB maximizes over x: the two branches cross at x = 1 − x/2 ⟹ x = 2/3, giving **c(1) = 2/3**. At this optimum all three final pieces (x/2, x/2, 1−x) are each exactly 1/3 — a clean "equalized" configuration. This matches the "minimax equalization" opening (#3 above).
  - **n=2 (strong evidence, verified to high numeric precision, matches an exact rational construction)**: LB marking at {1/7, 5/7} forces XY's best single-mark response to be at 2/7, producing final pieces {1/7, 1/7, 3/7, 2/7} (verified exactly: `lb_value` computed to full float precision gives 3/7+1/7 = 4/7 for LB). Two-mark XY responses (grid-searched) do no better than 4/7. A coarse global grid search over all (a,b) LB configurations found the same optimum value (≈0.571–0.573, matching 4/7=0.571428... within grid resolution). So **c(2) = 4/7**, i.e. LB's construction and XY's game value agree at 4/7, both numerically confirmed (not yet a hand-proof, but the exact rational match to 2 decimal places beyond grid noise is strong evidence, and the exact-fraction pieces {1/7,1/7,2/7,3/7} summing to 1 is a clean closed-form witness, not just a numeric coincidence).
  - **Conjectured closed form**: c(1)=2/3, c(2)=4/7 match the formula **c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} − 1)** (n=1: 2/3; n=2: 4/7; n=3 would predict 8/15). This was NOT independently checked at n=3 (time budget) — flagging as the single highest-value next computation (a 3-point-vs-3-point search, more expensive but tractable with the same greedy-claim-value code) to confirm or refute before the outliner commits to this formula. The recursive structure hints (n=2's optimal construction looks like an n=1 sub-pattern nested inside a larger piece — pieces 1/7,1/7,3/7,2/7 can be seen as: split off 1/7, leaving 6/7 which under a rescaled sub-game gives the 1/7,3/7,2/7 shares) support an inductive proof of the recursion c(n) in terms of c(n−1) rather than only checking the closed form by more small cases — this may be the fastest path to a real proof.
  - Denominators observed: 3, 7 = 2^2−1, 2^3−1, consistent with 2^{n+1}−1 growth; LB's numerator share is 2^n out of 2^{n+1}-1, i.e. LB keeps slightly more than half but the margin shrinks like 1/2 as n→∞ (c(n) → 1/2 from above), which is qualitatively sensible (more total marks for both sides should push the guaranteed value toward an even split, since XY gets more chances to equalize/steal, but LB, moving in the claim phase first, keeps a small persistent edge that decays geometrically rather than like 1/n).
