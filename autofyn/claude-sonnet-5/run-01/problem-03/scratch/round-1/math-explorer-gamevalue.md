### Small-case / intuition notes (rigor level marked)

**PROVEN (full proof below), not merely conjectured:** For any finite multiset of nonnegative reals `S = {a_1 ≥ a_2 ≥ ... ≥ a_k}`, define `f(S)` = the amount the player-to-move secures under optimal alternating claiming (each move: take one remaining piece; game value determined by backward induction — this recursion literally **is** the definition of optimal play in a finite deterministic zero-total-sum two player game with common knowledge of all values, so "the value" is unambiguous regardless of any assumed strategy). Then:
- `f(S) = a_1 + a_3 + a_5 + ⋯` (sum of odd-ranked elements, 1-indexed, sorted descending).
- Taking the current largest remaining piece is *always* an optimal move for the player to move (ties may give other optimal moves too, but greedy-max is always one of them). Hence "sort descending, alternate" gives the correct, forced value for **both** players simultaneously — this is the "common-valuation alternating selection" fact requested.

*Proof.* Strong induction on `k = |S|`. Base cases `k=0,1` trivial (`f=0`, `f=a_1`). For the inductive step, the recursion is: `f(S) = max_{x∈S} [x + (Σ(S) − x) − f(S\{x})] = Σ(S) − min_{x∈S} f(S\{x})` (removing `x` leaves the opponent to move on `S\{x}`, where they secure `f(S\{x})`, so the *mover* is left with `Σ(S\{x}) − f(S\{x})`, plus the piece `x` just taken). By the induction hypothesis, for `x = a_i`, `S\{a_i}` sorted descending is `a_1,...,a_{i-1},a_{i+1},...,a_k`, and
`f(S\{a_i}) = h(i) := Σ_{j<i, j odd} a_j + Σ_{j>i, j even} a_j`
(elements before position `i` keep their rank; elements after `i` shift down by one, flipping parity). Compute `h(i+1) − h(i)`: if `i` is odd this equals `a_i − a_{i+1} ≥ 0` (descending order); if `i` is even it equals `0`. So `h` is non-decreasing in `i`, hence minimized at `i=1`: `min_i h(i) = h(1) = a_2+a_4+⋯`. Therefore the mover's optimal choice is `x=a_1` (take the largest), and `f(S) = Σ(S) − h(1) = a_1+a_3+a_5+⋯`, closing the induction. ∎ *(Numerically cross-checked by brute-force minimax on 3000+ random integer multisets of size ≤7 — zero mismatches; the analytic proof above is the rigorous version, not just the numeric check.)*

**Consequence for the problem's structure:** the entire claiming phase collapses to arithmetic on the final piece-length multiset; the real content of the problem is the two-stage adversarial *cutting* game (phase 1). This is a genuine reduction that any approach should use as its opening move — approaches that instead try to reason about claiming *strategies* combinatorially (rather than reducing immediately to the odd/even-rank-sum formula) are wasting effort.

**Small `n` numeric probes on phase 1 (CONJECTURE, not proof):**
- `n=1`: exhaustive-enough numeric search over Liu Bang's single cut point `x` (giving pieces `x, 1-x`) and Xiang Yu's single response cut gives a clean, robust optimum at `x=1/3` (pieces `1/3, 2/3`), guaranteed value `2/3`. At this optimum Xiang Yu's best reply splits the larger `2/3`-piece into two `1/3`-pieces, producing final pieces `{1/3,1/3,1/3}` (a triple tie), giving Liu Bang `1/3+1/3 = 2/3` (odd ranks of 3 equal pieces). This value `2/3` is numerically robust (grid + optimizer agree tightly). Matches the candidate closed form `c(n) = (n+1)/(2n+1)` at `n=1` (giving `2/3`).
- `n=2`: noisy numerics (see Dead-ends caution above) found guaranteed values ranging ~0.55–0.60 across different candidate Liu Bang 3-piece splits, with the *best* found configuration numerically landing very close to `p≈0.29-0.31, q≈0.10-0.11, r≈0.59-0.60` giving value `≈0.6 = 3/5`, again matching `(n+1)/(2n+1)`. This is **suggestive but not confirmed** — the search is a non-convex nested min-max and got inconsistent results near the optimum depending on solver seed. Flag `c(n) = (n+1)/(2n+1)` as the leading conjecture to test/prove next (via an explicit construction, e.g. Liu Bang placing points so pieces are in a geometric-ish ratio forcing Xiang Yu toward an "all near-equal after best split" configuration — the `n=1` optimal-split structure, ending at 3 equal pieces after Xiang Yu's move, hints that Liu Bang's general strategy might aim to force, after Xiang Yu's optimal reply, a multiset that is "as equal as possible" among `2n+1` pieces — consistent with `(n+1)/(2n+1)` = fraction of odd ranks among `2n+1` equal pieces). A different natural guess to also rule out/in: `c(n) → 1/2` as `n→∞` is consistent with either `(n+1)/(2n+1)→1/2` or other forms, so large-`n` asymptotics alone won't discriminate; need `n=2` (or `n=3`) resolved analytically or with a much more careful/exact optimization (e.g. symbolic Lagrange conditions rather than black-box numeric optimizers) to confirm or refute `(n+1)/(2n+1)`.
