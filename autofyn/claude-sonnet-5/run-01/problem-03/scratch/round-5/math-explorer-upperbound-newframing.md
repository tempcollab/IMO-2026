## imo-2026-03

- Distinct openings:
  1. **(Upper-bound recursion, part 1 focus)** Numeric mark-by-mark reverse-engineering of the true optimal Xiang Yu strategy against several non-geometric configs at n=2,3 (see notes below) confirms the recursive DOM/HALVE picture qualitatively but reveals it is a genuinely *cascading* recursion (process the sorted list top-down, at each level choosing HALVE-or-DOM on the *current remaining tail's own top piece* with the *remaining* budget, not a single static rule applied once at the top) — and surfaces two concrete new mechanical facts not yet in the approach file: (a) Lemma DOM's boundary case `r=0` (i.e. `p_1 = S` exactly) needs only `k-1` marks, not `k`, which is exactly the "extra mark" the recursion needs at the next level down (confirmed on two independent 3- and 4-piece examples); (b) budget is sometimes left unused, and when the top two pieces are near-tied (`p_1 ≈ p_2`, `p_1 < 2p_2` and `p_1 < S`, so neither DOM nor HALVE's hypothesis fires at the top) the optimal move skips straight past `p_1,p_2` and acts on a *deeper* tail element instead — i.e. the recursion is not simply "always act on the current max," it can act on whichever level of the sorted list is farthest from a already-good local pairing. This is evidence the correct general theorem should be phrased as a strong induction on **(number of pieces, budget)** jointly (as flagged already in round 3's Rules memory point 15 re: the PEEL move), applied to the *whole* sorted list recursively — not induction on `n` with a single top-level split choice.
  2. **(New framing for the field)** Borrow the crux move from `aimo-0198` (Liar's guessing game): *"Bound a greedy minimizer's outcome by the average of its two available options, `min(A,B) ≤ (A+B)/2`, to get a clean recursive bound."* In our problem Xiang Yu is exactly a minimizer choosing between (at least) two known, fully-proved candidate moves — Lemma DOM (giving `oddrank = p_1` when `p_1≥S`) and Lemma HALVE (giving `oddrank = p_1/2 + oddrank(T')` when `p_1≥2p_2`) — so instead of casework pinning down *which* of DOM/HALVE (or a hybrid) is optimal in every regime, one could try to prove the **weaker but sufficient** statement `min(DOM_value, HALVE_value) ≤ c(n)·Σ(A)` directly via `(DOM_value+HALVE_value)/2 ≤ c(n)·Σ(A)` (an averaging/potential argument, no casework on which is truly minimal). This is a genuinely different proof *shape* (potential/averaging vs. exact-minimizer case analysis) that could be handed to an approach as `potential-averaging-bound`. Caveat (see below): a first numeric spot-check suggests the naive average of the two candidates is NOT always ≤ c(n)Σ(A) in the region where neither hypothesis (`p_1≥S` nor `p_1≥2p_2`) holds — so this framing likely needs a *third* candidate move (or the recursive/cascading version of DOM/HALVE rather than the flat one) plugged into the same averaging inequality, but the core technique (avoid exact-minimizer casework by bounding `min` via a convex combination of two *provably available* strategies) is a real, unexplored lever worth an approach slot.
  3. **(Alternative framing, LP/strategy-stealing)** `aimo-0560` (gardener/lumberjack, already flagged in memory) and `aimo-0225` (n-gon counter game, 2-adic-valuation halving recursion) both suggest recasting the state as an abstract multiset invariant and inducting on a single scalar derived from it (in `aimo-0225`'s case, `v_2` of a difference). For our problem the natural analogous invariant is the **number of "doubling levels"** in the current configuration relative to the geometric extremal `A_n` — i.e. measure how far `A` is from the self-similar geometric family via `k := max{i : p_i ≥ 2 p_{i+1} for all j≤i}` (the length of the initial HALVE-chain) and induct on `(n-k)`, the "distance to the pure-cascade case," rather than on `n` directly. Not developed further (per lens instructions), but a concrete alternative organizing variable to hand the outliner.
