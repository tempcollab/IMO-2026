## imo-2026-04

### Setup recap (from current.md + certified lemmas, not re-derived)
Triangle (p,q,r), p+q+r=180°. Cut vertex p at x1∈(0,p) gives children
A={q,x1,r+p−x1}, B={r,p−x1,q+x1}, always with (r+p−x1)+(q+x1)=180° (identity ★).
Certified: θ∈S ⟹ θ≤90°; {180/n : n≥2}⊆S via bisect+transfer+shift (all four
"forcing primitives" — bisection-double-hit p=2θ, altitude-double-hit θ=90°,
shift x1=θ, transfer x1=r+p−θ — are exhaustively classified: they are the ONLY
x1-values that put θ into a child at all). The open gap is necessity: is
S = {180/n} exactly, or could "free-choice" cuts (x1 not one of the four
primitives) let Mulan build a winning strategy for some other θ?

### Distinct openings (routes to necessity, avoiding the "closure operator C(V)" bug)

**Opening 1 — backward induction on the assumed finite strategy tree + a clean
field-theoretic (transcendence-degree) closure theorem.** Rather than forward-closing
a hand-built generator set (the bug in `corrected-genericity-bound`), assume θ∈S,
so a finite winning strategy tree T exists (depth N, every leaf contains θ,
every internal node's two children are subtrees of T or leaves). Do induction
on T from the leaves up. Key structural fact, already implicit in the cut
formula and NOT requiring case-enumeration: every child's "new" entry is
AFFINE in x1 with coefficient in {0,+1,−1} (A's third entry is r+p−x1, coeff
−1; B's second entry is p−x1, coeff −1; x1 itself appears with coeff +1 in A's
second slot and B's third slot q+x1). So if a leaf hits θ exactly on move k,
back-solving x1^(k) from "entry = θ" forces x1^(k) to be one of {θ, p−θ,
r+p−θ, θ−q} — reproducing the primitive classification, NOT by exhaustive case
search but by the coefficient-∈{0,±1} fact alone (cleaner, less bug-prone).
The harder, genuinely new part: for moves that do NOT hit θ immediately (used
to set up spectators etc.), pick Shan-Yu's start (p0,q0) as a pair
algebraically independent transcendentals over ℚ(θ), and argue by induction on
move number that whichever real number x1 Mulan picks (an ARBITRARY real in
(0,p), not assumed to be any nice function of p0,q0 — this is the point, it
sidesteps needing x1 to be "generic" or "algebraic"): if x1 is transcendental
over K_i := ℚ(θ, all angles present so far), then it strictly enlarges the
transcendence degree of the field generated by all future entries; since some
future leaf-entry must equal θ ∈ ℚ(θ) ⊆ K_0 exactly, and every entry is a
ℤ-affine tower in the x1's used along the path, an affine relation
c·x1^(k) + (K_i-element) = θ with c≠0 forces x1^(k) ∈ K_i already — i.e. any
x1 chosen transcendentally over K_i can contribute a *nonzero* coefficient
only to entries that are NEVER later forced to equal θ. This suggests (not yet
proved in full rigor) a genuine theorem: **WLOG every x1 along a winning path
lies in K_i** at the time it's chosen — this is exactly gap (i) flagged in
`binary-word-invariant` §5, and this framing attacks it head-on via
transcendence degree rather than "guess the closure by hand." Once x1∈K_i is
established, the remaining question — which K_i-valued x1 (not just the four
affine primitives) are useful — likely needs a second argument (e.g. that
nonlinear/rational-function x1's can be replaced WLOG by affine ones, perhaps
via a degree-counting argument on numerator/denominator degree in p0,q0).
**This is not fully closed, but it is a structurally different and more
robust route than re-deriving C(V) by hand** — it argues correctness via a
general field-theoretic principle instead of an enumerated closure, so it is
much less likely to silently omit a generator the way both prior "necessity"
attempts did (both times the bug was a missing generator in a hand-built
list).

**Opening 2 — p-adic / denominator-valuation obstruction (number-theoretic,
genuinely different tool).** All values reachable via the certified primitives
have denominators that are controlled by which primitives were used: bisection
introduces factors of 2 in the denominator (relative to the pre-bisection
value), shift/transfer only ever add/subtract θ or 180° (no denominator
growth beyond what θ already carries), transfer resets the third entry to
exactly 180°−θ (junk-free). This is structurally very close to the "denominator
matching" crux in `aimo-0355` (see below): that problem forces an integer
relation among a triangle's angles into a denominator/prime-support equality
between two rational numbers built from a triangle's side lengths, and solves
it via 2-adic argument (gcd bookkeeping) plus the fact that a monic integer
polynomial evaluated at p/q in lowest terms has denominator exactly q^k. Here,
an analogous discrete invariant to try: track v_2(denominator) of each present
angle when written as a rational multiple of 180° (whenever θ itself is
rational, θ=180a/b in lowest terms). Bisection multiplies v_2 by contributing
+1 to the "2-adic distance from being an odd-denominator combination of θ and
180"; shift/transfer never change the ODD part of the denominator. Conjecture
worth checking: **the odd part of the denominator (in lowest terms, as a
multiple of 180°) of every angle ever producible is forced, by the four
primitives, to divide the odd part of b (θ's own denominator)** — this would
directly force θ=180/n form once combined with the "no denominator growth"
fact for shift/transfer. This is a cleaner, purely arithmetic invariant
(no genericity / transcendence needed at all, so it sidesteps Opening 1's
open "WLOG x1∈K_i" question entirely for RATIONAL θ) but needs a separate
argument for irrational θ (where a genericity-style argument, or a much
simpler one — irrational θ can never be an integer affine combination of
rational p0,q0,180° unless the θ-coefficient is forced to a specific value —
is probably needed anyway, and is easier than the rational case). **Promising
because it only needs elementary number theory (denominators / v_2), not
field-extension machinery — worth the outliner trying as a parallel, simpler
route for the rational-θ sub-case**, with Opening 1 (or the existing
transcendence heuristic) handling irrational θ.

**Opening 3 — operationalize the adversary directly as an explicit Shan-Yu
discard rule (not a "Mulan can't win" non-existence argument).** Define
Shan-Yu's full strategy explicitly: (a) start with p0,q0 chosen as
algebraically independent transcendentals over ℚ(θ) (or, per Opening 2, chosen
with denominator odd-part coprime to θ's); (b) after any Mulan cut, Shan-Yu
computes both children's angle-triples EXACTLY (as elements of the field/ring
generated by the game so far) and discards according to the rule "keep
whichever child does NOT contain θ; if both avoid θ, keep either (say, the one
whose 'new' entry has strictly positive coefficient of the transcendental
part, to keep bookkeeping simple)." The proof obligation becomes purely
mechanical: show this SPECIFIC rule, applied forever, never produces θ. This
reframes necessity as a **loop invariant proof for one fixed algorithm**
rather than a universal quantifier over all Mulan strategies — often easier to
get right (fewer places to hide a missing case) since you just need to show
the invariant "no present angle's formula, as a K_i-element, equals θ" is
preserved by every possible legal cut (this is really the same content as
Opening 1 but organized as an explicit invariant-preservation proof, which is
a more standard/lower-risk proof shape for this kind of adversary argument —
this is the shape used successfully in `aimo-0236`'s valuation-nursing games
and `aimo-0225`'s 2-adic-valuation game-value recursion, see below).

**Opening 4 (weaker, flagged as likely subsumed) — pure current-state
monovariant with no memory of p0,q0.** Tried mentally: define Φ(triangle) =
some function of the current three angles alone (e.g., "is some angle exactly
kθ for integer k", or minimal |a−mθ| over integers m). Doesn't obviously work
because whether an angle is "dangerous" depends on its algebraic relationship
to the ORIGINAL junk (p0,q0), which a pure current-state function can't see
without also tracking history/genericity — i.e. Opening 4 collapses into
Openings 1–3 once you try to make it rigorous. Not recommended as a
free-standing route; mention only so the outliner doesn't waste a round
rediscovering this dead end.

### Analogous past problems (cruxes)
- `aimo-0355` (number_theory / modular-arithmetic-and-CRT + p-adic-valuation,
  from `past_crux_moves_database.json`) — **best analogue found**. Problem:
  triangle with integer sides n−1,n,n+1 is "quirky" if some nontrivial integer
  relation r1θ1+r2θ2+r3θ3=0 holds among its angles; find all such n. Crux move:
  use the angle-sum constraint to eliminate one angle from the integer
  relation, reducing to an equality of cosines of integer multiples of two
  angles; express cos(kθ) via the integer Chebyshev-like polynomial P_k
  (X^k+X^{-k}=P_k(X+1/X)); then use that a monic integer polynomial evaluated
  at a reduced fraction p/q has denominator EXACTLY q^k, forcing the two
  sides' denominators to share prime support, which (combined with gcd
  bookkeeping) collapses to a small finite check. This is directly analogous
  in SPIRIT to what necessity here needs: turn "does an exact rational-affine
  coincidence with θ occur" into a denominator/prime-support argument instead
  of a hand-built closure — i.e. it is a worked example of Opening 2's
  strategy (denominator-valuation obstruction) succeeding on a genuinely
  similar-flavored triangle-angle rational-relation problem. Worth the
  outliner reading `past_problems_database.json`'s aimo-0355 solution in full
  before attempting Opening 2.
- `aimo-0225` (combinatorics / games-and-strategy) — two-player game on a
  regular n-gon with counters; game value determined by recursing on the
  2-adic valuation of a difference that halves at each relevant step (P/N
  status flips with the valuation's parity). Analogous in flavor to our
  bisection-halves-everything structure, but the game mechanics (discrete
  n-gon slides, not continuous cevian cuts with adversarial discard) are quite
  different — useful mainly as a template for "recurse on v_2 of a
  difference," a technique that could inform a cleaner statement of Opening
  2's denominator argument, but not directly transplantable.
- `aimo-0296` (combinatorics / invariants-and-monovariants) — "force a target
  value of an integer-quantized quantity by a discrete intermediate-value
  argument: exhibit an operation that negates it and a move that changes it by
  at most one quantum, then walk between two matched endpoints." Interesting
  general pattern (IVT-style forcing) but for a SUFFICIENCY-style forcing
  argument, not necessity/obstruction; not a strong match for the current gap
  (necessity), flagging only because it's the closest "games-and-strategy +
  invariants" hit in the corpus for continuous/angle-flavored forcing.
- No crux was found that handles a *continuous* (real-parameter cevian cut)

  mechanics (continuum of legal moves x1∈(0,p), adversary discards one of two

Necessity (S ⊆ {180°/n}) is the sole open gap; the four move primitives

### Dead ends (do not retry)
- `corrected-genericity-bound`'s hand-built closure operator C(V) using only
  {halve, reflect} generators — refuted (missing the shift generator; a real
  θ=180/7° counterexample was hand-verified). Per run_state.md's standing
  rule, ANY closure-operator-style necessity argument must be checked against
  the FULL primitive list (bisection, altitude-double-hit, shift, transfer) —
  not a subset chosen because it "felt complete."
- `full-interval-hypothesis` — investigated whether S = (0°,90°] (i.e. no real
  restriction beyond non-obtuseness); refuted by the existence of the
  {180/n} lower bound family combined with the fact its own restricted
  "bisection + cross-transfer" move family (omitting shift) gave a false
  negative on 180/7°. Not a necessity argument as such, just establishes H1
  (full interval) is not obviously true; doesn't bear directly on the current
  gap either way.
- Opening 4 above (pure current-state monovariant, no history/genericity) —
  self-assessed as a dead end on reflection (not previously attempted by name
  in the workspace, but flagged here so it isn't independently rediscovered
  and burn a round): it cannot distinguish "this specific numeric angle
  equals θ" from "this angle's FORMULA in terms of the original junk equals
  θ" without carrying history, which is exactly what genericity/transcendence
  or denominator-tracking (Openings 1–2) already do properly.
