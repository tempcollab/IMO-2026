### Converse direction — rotation/equidistribution framing (the distinct idea here)

**Motivating observation.** The single deterministic "chip" move (split a vertex of angle
`s>θ` at `x1=θ`, forcing Shan-Yu into `Child2=(c,s-θ,b+θ)`) transfers exactly `θ` between two
angles while leaving the third fixed. Repeated chipping *on a fixed pair of vertices* is
formally the same recurrence as iterating the map `s ↦ s-θ (mod nothing, just real subtraction)`
— i.e., watching one coordinate decrease by fixed steps of size θ. If `θ` were an *irrational*
multiple of some natural period (there is no forced periodicity here since angles are bounded to
`(0,180)`, not literally on a circle) the relevant classical tool is the **three-distance
theorem** (Steinhaus): the points `{θ, 2θ, 3θ, ..., kθ} mod L` (for any real period `L`) partition
the interval `[0,L)` into at most 3 distinct gap lengths, and in particular `kθ` never lands
exactly on a prescribed target `θ' ∈ (0,L)` unless `θ'` is itself a rational multiple of `θ`
compatible with `L` — this is the natural tool for ruling out *exact* hits under repeated
addition, and is the standard technique (see knowledge_base.md "Invariants & monovariants",
and the general engineering of avoidance arguments in discrete/number-theoretic games).

**Proposed mechanism.** Fix `θ` with `180/θ∉ℤ`. Shan-Yu's strategy: start with an equilateral
triangle `(60,60,60)` — no, better, start with a triangle whose two free angles are chosen so
that the "reachable value set" (all angles ever expressible via the master formula's repeated
`±θ`, `±(kept angle)` operations) stays within a set that provably never contains θ exactly.
Formally: consider the additive group generated by `θ` and `180` inside `ℝ`, i.e.
`G = θℤ + 180ℤ`. If `180/θ` is irrational, `G` is dense in `ℝ` (two incommensurable generators) —
density does NOT immediately give avoidance (in fact it suggests values *can* get arbitrarily
close to θ, though not proven to hit it exactly — exact equality is what matters here, and a
dense but countable set missing a specific point is generic, consistent with survival). If
`180/θ = p/q` is rational but `q∤` ... i.e. `180/θ` is a non-integer rational `p/q` (lowest
terms, `q≥2`), then `G = (θ/q)·... ` — need care: `G=θℤ+180ℤ`; since `180=θ·(p/q)`,
`G = θ·(ℤ + (p/q)ℤ) = θ·(1/q)ℤ` (as `gcd(p,q)=1` implies `ℤ+(p/q)ℤ=(1/q)ℤ`) — so `G` is the
discrete group generated by `θ/q`. Since `q≥2` here (as `180/θ` is not an integer), `θ` is *not*
itself a generator of `G` at "level 1" — rather `θ=q·(θ/q)`, i.e. θ is the `q`-th multiple of the
group's true generator. **This is the key number-theoretic fact distinguishing the winning case
from the losing case**: when `180/θ=n∈ℤ` (i.e. `q=1`), θ generates `G` directly (`G=θℤ`, and
`180=nθ∈G` trivially, consistent with Mulan's construction using exactly multiples of θ);
when `180/θ=p/q` with `q≥2` (whether or not `p=1`), the natural "resonance lattice" `G` is
generated by `θ/q`, a STRICTLY finer spacing than θ, and 180 sits at the `p`-th multiple of this
finer generator, not reachable via θ-steps alone landing back exactly on a θ-multiple of a
generic starting point — this is the mechanism that should make exact-hit forcing fail to be
*universal* (work for ALL starting triangles) even though individual coincidences (like the
`θ=60°,(20,50,110)` example) can still occur for special starting angles.

**Gap (the real content, not yet proven)**: turn the above heuristic lattice discussion into an
actual induction showing NO finite Mulan strategy from a *suitably chosen* starting triangle
forces θ, when `180/θ∉ℤ`. The three-distance theorem's role would be to bound, for a
`chip`-only sub-strategy, how close the iterates `s-kθ` (or `s+kθ`) can come to a specific target
without exact equality (three-distance controls gaps between visited points on a circle of
period `L`, but our "circle" here would need to be artificially constructed — angles live in
`(0,180)`, not literally `mod L` for a natural `L` tied to θ, since triangle angles do not wrap
around). **Honest assessment**: this equidistribution framing is the LEAST developed of the
three approaches and its applicability is not yet confirmed — the "angles don't literally live
on a circle" mismatch is a real obstacle (three-distance theorem needs a genuine circle/modular
structure, and it's not obvious the triangle-angle game has one, since angles are bounded and
absorbed by the OTHER two angles via the `a+b+c=180` constraint rather than wrapping). **Before
investing further, the next builder pass on this approach should first check on a concrete
example (e.g. θ=50°, `180/50=3.6=18/5` so `p=18,q=5`) whether the "generator = θ/q" idea
actually predicts anything checkable against the explorer's numerical sweep** (explorer-verify
found θ=50° fails to produce a forced win on generic test triangles) — if it does not cleanly
explain that data point, this approach should be abandoned in favor of the linear-independence
genericity argument in chip-double-force.md, which has a cleaner (if still incomplete) mechanism.
