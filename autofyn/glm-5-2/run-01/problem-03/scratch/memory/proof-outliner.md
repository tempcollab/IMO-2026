ALWAYS: verify the lower-bound "resistance" lemma with an actual counterexample-search before accepting a generic structural label (because "superincreasing" sounded right but {3,1}/4 refuted it — the full dyadic tower is special, round 1 imo-2026-03).

NEVER: assume the "rest of an arbitrary config rescales to a subgame" for self-similar lower-bound towers — the self-similarity is exact only for the tower itself; arbitrary Liu configs' remainders have no tower structure (round 1 imo-2026-03).

NEVER: trust an explorer's "parity-flip defeats the conjecture" claim without checking it against the DYADIC (tower) config, not just a near-dyadic one (round 1 imo-2026-03: corpus explorer's flip only beat {1,2,3}/11, the dyadic {1,2,4,...}/15 is immune).

NEVER: open a standalone slug for a "scaffold, not standalone" framing that an explorer explicitly flagged as unable to close the gap alone — fold it into the slug it scaffolds instead, else it shares the same wall and dies alone (round 2 imo-2026-03: the binary-tree/frontier framing's non-monotonicity + unbalanced-split gap = the same non-dyadic-breakpoint wall as the variational route; folded into tower-induction).

ALWAYS: retire a slug whose load-bearing step is the bound restated (circular) rather than build it — but harvest its one solid sound lemma into the shared cache first (round 2 imo-2026-03: balanced-configs B3 "check every type ≤ bound" IS the bound; Lemma B1 "optimum at balanced/tie refinement" is sound and importable).

NEVER: open TWO upper-bound slugs that both ultimately need "the tower is the worst config / monotonicity" — pick ONE and fold the other's clean partial lemmas in as proven scaffolding, with its open sub-case noted as a fallback within the same slug (round 2 imo-2026-03: inductive-upper and majorization-upper share the exchange-monotonicity wall; merged into majorization-upper).

ALWAYS: when a live approach's wall is confirmed shared across slugs (opposite machinery, same gap), keep both live and diversify WITHIN them (different sub-routes on the same wall) rather than spawning a third rival slug on the same wall — a third slug would be a single-gap trap (round 3 imo-2026-03: tail-count PL vs tower-induction block on G1-lower; the genuinely-new slug went to a DIFFERENT framing [gaps+leftover charging], not a third lens on the same D-integral).
ALWAYS: when an explorer reports a conjecture strongly verified but unproved (e.g. the Max-bound D*<=M/2^n), center the revised slug on it as the new spine but mark the unproved sub-step as an explicit GAP — do not present the conjecture as proven scaffolding (round 3 imo-2026-03: majorization-upper revised to Max-bound, the non-dominant a_3>a_1/2 sub-step is the explicit GAP).
ALWAYS: when an explorer decisively kills a route (e.g. majorization/Schur-convexity with counterexamples), DROP it from the revised slug's spine — do not keep the dead route as a fallback within the same framing (round 3 imo-2026-03: majorization KILLED by single-piece (1) most-majorizing yet D*=0; dropped, not retained).
NEVER: present the Max-bound D*<=M/2^n as a live spine — it is FALSE (round 4 exact counterexample (7,6,5,3)/21 n=3, D*=1/21>M/8=1/24, ratio 8/7; a whole family of near-tower violators). The actual upper-bound target D*<=1/D_n is still unviolated; only the Max-bound conjecture is wrong. Replace the majorization-upper spine with V(n)=M_2/2^{n-1} (second-largest piece), which is violation-free and tight at the tower. Record the counterexample in Approaches tried.
ALWAYS: when a conjecture (Max-bound) is falsified but the ANSWER stands, distinguish sharply — the conjecture was a sufficient-not-necessary lemma; the answer c(n) is still correct because the real target is weaker. Do not let a falsified sufficient lemma cascade into doubting the answer (round 4 imo-2026-03).
ALWAYS: for the lower-bound non-dyadic multi-split wall, the simultaneous multi-coordinate transport (NOT per-split local rebalancing) is the mechanism — the min-level set {D=1} is star-shaped w.r.t. the dyadic attainer, proved via the telescoping zero-gradient block condition (each split's two fragments at same-sign positions ⇒ gradient 0 ⇒ D constant on the cell). The V-shape blocks ONLY single-coordinate moves; the transport goes ALONG PL cell faces (tie loci), around the V-shape (round 4 imo-2026-03, 816/816 T_3 cascade verified).
NEVER: open two lower-bound slugs that both bottom out on "the min-level set contains a dyadic config" — but the LP/Farkas-dual framing is genuinely orthogonal: it bottoms out on "a feasible sign-pattern always exists" (linear-algebra feasibility), NOT on the primal position-sign property. The plateau transport (geometric/PL star-shaped) and the LP-dual (linear-algebra feasibility) attack DIFFERENT walls on the same goal — both are live (round 4 imo-2026-03).
ALWAYS: the LP-dual of Xiang's refinement-min LP (weights on bin-sum + sort constraints) is NON-circular — its dual objective Σ_t y_eq[t]·2^{n-t} uses tower bin VALUES only, never sorted positions. This is different from the round-3 claim-game dual (weights on pieces = re-deriving odd-index, circular). Verify non-circularity explicitly when opening an LP-dual slug.

ALWAYS: when two lower-bound framings converge on the SAME closing lemma (round 5: the spine sign-pattern lemma — PL/variational tail-count, LP/Farkas lp-dual, spine/bookkeeping tower-induction, charging gaps-leftover ALL need it), they are a single-gap trap UNLESS they attack it via genuinely different PROOF MECHANISMS (combinatorial subset-sum vs LP feasibility/Farkas vs spine-value arithmetic). Keep at most 2-3 rival-mechanism slugs live; HOLD the rest. If all stall on the same lemma for 3 rounds, retire all but the strongest mechanism (round 5 imo-2026-03).

NEVER: chase an inductive IH "crux regime" (e.g. V(n)←V(n−1) overshooting 37%) before computing the actual D* in that regime — the IH is a worst-case bound BLIND to slack, so a "37% failure" of the IH can be a phantom crux where D*=0 or tiny (round 5 imo-2026-03 upper explorer: the V(n≥4) crux regime a_1<2a_2∧a_3>a_1/2 has D*=0 or ratio ≤0.52; the hard case is the DOMINANT tower-tail family where the winning move is halving, not pairing). ALWAYS: when an explorer pins the obstruction to "a missing finite template-classification" (NOT a genuinely-hard expense), center the revised slug on that single lemma and identify the EXACT hard sub-step — the multi-swap subset-sum argument, not the single-swap (which is often already rigorous) (round 5 imo-2026-03 nosaddle explorer: single-swap impossibility t≠v is rigorous; the multi-swap subset-sum is the load-bearing unproved step; the naive value-type argument fails in isolation since 3=2+1).

ALWAYS: when a cheap certifiable milestone collapses a large sub-case unconditionally (e.g. `halving-always-a-nplus1`: halving a_1..a_n gives D=a_{n+1} for ANY strictly-decreasing m=n+1 config, by a parity/grouping argument — drops a hypothesis and closes the a_{n+1}≤1/D_n region for ALL n), fold it into the advancing slug's first step AND list it as a certifiable sub-task the builder must promote to the lemma cache this round (round 6 imo-2026-03: this halving lemma verified 0/20000, generalizes `bottom-dominant-halving`, narrows GAP-U2 to the single "compressed" case a_{n+1}>1/D_n).

NEVER: open THREE slugs on the same upper-wall sub-case (e.g. the compressed case a_{n+1}>1/D_n) — routes O1 (IVT/split-bottom), O2 (split-large-to-match-medium subset-sum), and bounded-spread pigeonhole ALL bottom on the SAME subset-sum/multiset-equal-sums existence core, so they form a single-gap trap; open at most ONE of them as a slug and fold the others as a fallback within it.
