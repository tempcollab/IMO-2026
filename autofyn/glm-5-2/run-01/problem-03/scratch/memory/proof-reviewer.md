# proof-reviewer per-role rules

ALWAYS: independently re-derive the load-bearing lemma by hand/computation (the tail-count approach's Lemma 0 had a sign error — formula `T_1−T_j = a_1+(a_3−a_2)+…` is WRONG, correct is `(a_1−a_2)+(a_3−a_4)+…`; the conclusion held but the written proof was buggy). A correct lemma can have a buggy proof — certify via the cleanest write-up, note the defect in the others. (round 1)

ALWAYS: when verifying tower-resistance numerically, build the tower with ALL n+1 pieces `(2^n,…,2,1)/D_n` — `range(n,-1,-1)` not `range(n,0,-1)`. A truncated tower (missing the `1` piece) produces fake violations and falsely impugns the approaches. (round 1)

NEVER: certify a lemma as standalone if its proof depends on an unproved IH. tower-induction's equal-split sub-case (b-i) is closed only conditionally on the lower bound for n−1 — it is a reduction, not an unconditional result; reject it as a standalone promotable lemma. (round 1)

ALWAYS: when a proof's casework has a "sub-case" that is actually VACUOUS (e.g. majorization-upper n=2 regime C1: a_1<4/7 & a_2>=2a_3 is empty because sum<1), the displayed inequality chain inside the vacuous case is still a rigor defect (a wrong sign/direction) — flag it, but verify the case is empty before impugning the conclusion. (round 2, majorization-upper C1)

NEVER: accept "exhaustive grid search" over a continuous split-position space as a PROOF, even at fine resolution — a grid can miss breakpoints between grid points. The PL+breakpoint reduction is a proof only when the (finite, exactly enumerable) breakpoint configs are enumerated, not grid-sampled. Flag grid-verified claims as evidence, not proof. (round 2, tail-count "n=2,3 rigorously established")

ALWAYS: deduplicate lemma certifications across slugs — two builders often prove the same fact (tail-count's balanced-split-frontier-recursion == tower-induction's F-rec special case; tail-count §5 == tower-induction F-min; tail-count §6 == majorization-upper B1). Certify ONCE under a canonical general statement, note the others as equivalent/specializations. (round 2)

ALWAYS: when a lemma's case analysis claims "Cases 1–k cover all breakpoint types," independently enumerate the breakpoint types (pairs of independent tie conditions on the cut variables: cut-tower-tie, cut-fragment-tie, fragment-tower-tie, fragment-fragment-tie) and check each is addressed. The two-split-lower-bound lemma claimed exhaustiveness but missed Type-4 (r-tower-tie) breakpoints — they reduced to Case 1 by config-structure symmetry, but the proof didn't say so. Verify the conclusion computationally (Fraction-exact) for ALL breakpoint types, not just the proved ones; flag missing cases as a rigor defect even if the conclusion holds. (round 3, two-split-lower-bound)

NEVER: certify a conditional reduction as a standalone lemma just because the builder placed the file in `lemmas/` — check whether the proof depends on an unproved IH. The Max-bound MB-Dom/MB-Pair reductions depend on the Max-bound conjecture `W(n−1)` (itself unproved for `n ≥ 3`); they must be marked "REDUCTION (NOT standalone certified)" in the file header so importers don't treat the conditional as proved. Consistent with round-2 U2/U3 treatment. (round 3, majorization-upper)

ALWAYS: when two builders independently prove the same sub-result (e.g. tail-count §8 even-group pair-cancellation via PL/variational, tower-induction S3 via spine decomposition), certify BOTH lemmas and note they are independent proofs of the same fact — this strengthens the cache (two routes to the same result, one may generalize further). (round 3, even-group-spine-lower-bound vs tail-count §8)

NEVER: approve a "V(3) provable from the certified n=2 base" claim without checking BOTH regimes — pairing a_1 (non-dominant a_1<2a_2) puts two a_2's at positions 1,2 and cancels, but in the DOMINANT case a_1>=2a_2 pairing puts a_1-a_2 >= a_2 at position 1 and the bound fails; the dominant case needs HALVING (the MB-Dom n=3 instance). The V(3) proof is a two-case split, not a single pairing move. Verify the witness (8,3,2,2)/15: pairing gives D=1/3 (FAILS), halving gives rest max=M_2 (WORKS). (round 4, majorization-upper V(3))

ALWAYS: when an LP/Farkas-dual approach derives its dual, independently verify the dual with scipy (primal min == dual max) on a concrete cell — the lp-dual-certificate LP-2 had a SIGN ERROR in the mountain (claimed nonneg prefix sums of d_k=y_eq[b(k)]-(−1)^k; correct is nonpos, or equivalently d_k=(−1)^k-y_eq[b(k)] for nonneg). The builder's interleaved T_2 cert (objective 2) was INFEASIBLE (actual LP min=1, correct dual max=1, violating strong duality). Clean-types (y_ub=0) was unaffected. Always cross-check dual feasibility by solving both primal and dual numerically. (round 4, lp-dual-certificate LP-2)

NEVER: let a builder claim "c(n) = r_n" (equality) by combining a PROVED upper bound with a PARTIAL lower bound — the n=3 upper bound c(3)<=8/15 is proved (V(3) + four-regime closure), but the n=3 LOWER bound is still partial (GAP-C open even for n=3). Flag "c(3) = 8/15" as an overclaim; the correct statement is "c(3) <= 8/15 proved, equality pending the lower bound." (round 4, majorization-upper)

ALWAYS: when a builder flags a "mass identity" or "sign-pattern lemma" as the G1-closer, independently re-derive the decomposition `D = (F−T) + 2(correction term)` and check whether the proposed identity is just `D=1` restated under the assumed pattern — the spine sign-pattern / multi-swap subset-sum framing for GAP-C was CIRCULAR (round 5: "F = T+1" ≡ D=1 under the interleaving pattern; spine `{5,2}` from T_3 has the pattern but F−T=3≠1). A valuable negative result.

ALWAYS: for LP-dual sign conventions, re-derive from standard form (min primal with A_ub p ≤ b_ub ⇒ dual y_ub ≤ 0) and verify with scipy.optimize.linprog marginals — the per-type refinement-min LP is NOT totally unimodular (min D real: 5/3, 13/3, 17/3 for n=3), so the parity "D≠0" sub-result is rigorous but insufficient; never accept "objective ≥ 1" as a shortcut around G1 (strong duality makes it EQUIVALENT). (round 5)

NEVER: accept a "characterizes D=1 cells" lemma as proving "D ≥ 1 everywhere" — the mass-balance lemma (D=2S₊−D_n on block cells) is a genuine characterization (sub-gap (ii) vacuous) but explicitly does NOT prove D≥1 on V-shape cells or all-top-`−` block cells; read the caveat. (round 5)

ALWAYS: flag any approach claiming "tower tightness PROVED both directions" — the LOWER direction (D*(T_n) ≥ 1/D_n) IS the open GAP-C (the tower lower bound), NOT certified. The upper direction (D*(T_n) ≤ 1/D_n, halving) is certified. Round 6 even-packing-upper overclaimed "for the TOWER specifically the lower bound is certified closed" — this is FALSE; GAP-C is exactly the open tower lower bound. Do not let an upper-bound approach piggyback on a lower-bound result it doesn't own. (round 6)

NEVER: accept a trivial change-of-variables (D = 1−2E ⟺ D* ≤ 1/D_n ⟺ E* ≥ (2^n−1)/D_n) as a "genuinely different mechanism" — it is a RESTATE, not a bypass. The load-bearing proof step (exhibit marks creating ties/pairings with small leftover) is identical. Flag the approach honestly: reframe + diagnostic are certifiable scaffolding, but the core gap is unchanged. (round 6, even-packing-upper)

ALWAYS: when verifying "block condition holds at all D=1 breakpoints," check the classification method — classify spine pieces by ORIGIN (fragment of top 2^n vs tower piece below top), NOT by value-type (power of 2 vs not). A fragment of the top can be a power of 2 (e.g. 4 from splitting 8→4+4); value-type classification misclassifies it as a "tower piece" and gives false "block condition OK" results. The round-5 "failures" were this misclassification bug; the round-6 origin-based correction is right. (round 6)

ALWAYS: the parity obstruction (2n+1 odd ⟹ D≠0) applies ONLY when using exactly n marks. Using ≤ n−1 marks gives an even piece count, allowing D=0 via all-pair-cancellation. Do not flag "D*=0 for compressed configs" as wrong — it can be correct (via ≤ n−1 marks). Verify the mark count in the witness strategy. (round 6)
