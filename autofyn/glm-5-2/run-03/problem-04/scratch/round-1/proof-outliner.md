## imo-2026-04

Conjectured answer (all three explorers, computational + structural): **Mulan wins ⟺ θ = 180°/n for an integer n ≥ 2.** Each slug below is a whole attempt at BOTH directions of this characterization. The cut operation (verified by all three explorers) is shared infrastructure: cutting to vertex A (other angles B, C) with parameter α ∈ (0, A) yields children C1 = (α, B, 180−α−B) and C2 = (A−α, C, B+α); the two fresh P-angles (180−α−B) and (B+α) are supplementary (sum 180°). Reparametrizing β = B+α ∈ (B, 180−C), the P-angles are β and 180−β. KB entries in play: *Invariants & monovariants*, *Induction / infinite descent*, *Pigeonhole / extremal*, *Casework / exhaustion*.

### lattice-descent: new
Target: Prove Mulan wins ⟺ θ = 180°/n (n ≥ 2 integer). Both directions via the discrete "taint = integer multiple of θ" invariant on the lattice L_n.
Technique: Invariants & monovariants + Induction/infinite-descent on the integer *level* m of a tracked angle = mθ. The cleanest, most-validated route (lattice explorer, 400/400 sim).
Skeleton:
  1. Define taint: angle x is tainted iff x = kθ for some integer k ≥ 1. For θ = 180°/n, L_n = {(aθ, bθ, cθ) : a+b+c = n, a,b,c ≥ 1} is closed under cuts with α = (integer)·θ.
  2. **Reduce move (M2)** — works for ANY θ: if a triangle has an angle mθ (m ≥ 2) at vertex V, cut to V with α = θ. Then C1 = (θ, …) contains θ (immediate threat); C2 = ((m−1)θ, …) carries the reduced multiple. Shan-Yu must keep C2 to avoid θ; the tracked level m → m−1. Iterate to m = 1 (win). Potential = m; strictly decreases; bound ≤ m−1. — This is the engine that makes "any tainted angle" a forced win, so Shan-Yu must avoid ALL multiples, not just θ.
  3. **Inclusion — Alignment move (M1)** (θ = 180°/n, n ≥ 3): If no angle is a multiple of θ, Mulan cuts to the largest angle A. Since A ≥ 60° ≥ 180°/n = θ (equality only equilateral n=3, already won), the open interval (B, A+B) has length A > θ, so by pigeonhole it contains a multiple kθ (1 ≤ k ≤ n−1). Set α = kθ − B. Then C2's P-angle = B+α = kθ; C1's P-angle = 180° − kθ = (n−k)θ. Both children carry an exact multiple of θ. Whichever Shan-Yu keeps, run (M2) and descend to θ. Total ≤ 1 + (n−2) = n−1 moves.
  4. **Inclusion — boundary n = 2 (θ = 90°):** Cut to the largest angle's vertex; its other two angles B, C are acute (sum < 90 each, since A ≥ 90). Set β = 90° (i.e. α = 90° − B). Both P-angles = 90°; one-move win. (M1 needs A > θ = 90°, which can fail for acute triangles, hence the separate case.)
  5. **Exclusion — 2×2 taint casework** (θ ≠ 180°/n, incl. irrational): Shan-Yu maintains invariant I = "no angle of T is an integer multiple of θ." Initial triangle: pick one avoiding the finite forbidden set {kθ : 1 ≤ k ≤ ⌊180/θ⌋} (possible; the set is finite for every θ > 0). To show I is preservable, prove Mulan cannot taint BOTH children from a taint-free parent (cut to A; B, C, A all untainted by I). Four cases on (how C1 is tainted) × (how C2 is tainted):
     - α-slot & α-slot: α = k₁θ and A−α = k₂θ ⇒ A = (k₁+k₂)θ, contradicting A untainted. ✗
     - α-slot (C1) & P-slot (C2): α = k₁θ and B+α = k₂θ ⇒ B = (k₂−k₁)θ, contradicting B untainted. ✗
     - P-slot (C1) & α-slot (C2): 180−B−α = k₁θ (α = 180−B−k₁θ) and A−α = k₂θ ⇒ C = (k₁−k₂)θ, contradicting C untainted. ✗
     - P-slot & P-slot: 180−B−α = k₁θ and B+α = k₂θ ⇒ (k₁+k₂)θ = 180°, i.e. θ = 180°/(k₁+k₂). For θ ≠ 180°/n this has no integer solution (for irrational θ, no integers k₁+k₂ make θ rational — immediate ✗). ✗
     All four fail, so at least one child is taint-free; Shan-Yu keeps it; I preserved; no angle ever equals θ (= 1·θ). Finite rigorous casework = the exclusion's load-bearing wall.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - **Reduce move descends level m → m−1** — because cutting the mθ-vertex with α = θ puts θ in C1 (threat) and (m−1)θ in C2, forcing Shan-Yu into C2; validity rests on (m−1)θ > 0 and the preserved angle + P-angle staying positive (inherited from parent).
  - **Alignment move exists iff 180 = nθ** — because the two P-angles are supplementary (β, 180−β); making BOTH multiples of θ requires β = kθ and 180−β = (n−k)θ, summing to nθ = 180.
  - **Taint-free invariant is closed** — because the four ways to taint both children each reduce to a linear relation expressing a parent angle (or 180) as an integer combination of θ, contradicting the parent's taint-freedom unless θ = 180/n.
  - **"Any tainted angle ⇒ forced win"** — because (M2) reduces m → 1 in m−1 forced moves regardless of θ; this is why Shan-Yu's invariant must forbid ALL multiples, not just θ.
Open gaps (builder fills):
  - Tracked-vertex formalization in (M2): define the tracked vertex invariantly (e.g. "the angle that is a positive multiple of θ and was most recently created/reduced by the alignment move"), prove it stays locatable through each descent step, and prove that committing to ONE tracked vertex terminates even if other multiples appear simultaneously (the explorer noted an early greedy that switched multiples cycled; the fix is commitment).
  - Positivity verification for (M1): confirm both kθ and (n−k)θ lie in (0, 180) and all six child angles are positive (1 ≤ k ≤ n−1, n ≥ 3).
  - n = 2 boundary: verify (M1)'s A > θ = 90° assumption genuinely fails for acute triangles and the β = 90° fork covers every non-right triangle (and a right triangle is already won).
  - Irrational-θ arm of the casework: state cleanly that the 4th case has no solution since (k₁+k₂)θ = 180° would force θ rational; and the initial triangle exists because the forbidden set, while possibly large, is finite (⌊180/θ⌋ values) so its complement in the angle-simplex is nonempty (indeed dense).
  - θ ∈ (90°, 180°): confirm excluded (no n ≥ 2 gives 180/n > 90); the 4th case forces θ = 180/(k₁+k₂) ≤ 90 for k₁+k₂ ≥ 2.
Cases to cover: n = 2 (θ = 90°, one-move fork); n ≥ 3 (alignment + descent); θ irrational; θ = 180·(p/q) with p > 1 (rational non-divisor); θ > 90°.
Watch out for: the (M2) tracked-vertex commitment is the most likely place to lose rigor (a hand-wavy "track the multiple" without an invariant definition); the 4th-case casework must handle θ irrational and θ = 180·p/q (p > 1) explicitly, not just integer-degree θ; do NOT assume the initial triangle is on L_n (exclusion must pick a taint-free off-lattice start).

### euclidean-needle: new
Target: Prove Mulan wins ⟺ θ = 180°/n. Inclusion via needle-attraction + subtractive-Euclidean extraction on the large angle; exclusion via the Euclidean remainder δ = 180 mod θ blocking termination.
Technique: Induction/infinite-descent on the sorted angle triple (needle phase) + the subtractive Euclidean algorithm on (large angle, 180) (extraction phase). Genuinely different inclusion mechanism from lattice-descent: no alignment-to-lattice move; instead drive to a canonical thin needle then run Euclid.
Skeleton:
  1. Cut operation; the two P-angles are supplementary (β, 180−β) with β = B+α ∈ (B, 180−C).
  2. **Inclusion phase 1 — Needle attraction:** From any triangle, Mulan repeatedly cuts to the smallest angle (call it s) with α = ε (arbitrarily small). One child is a thin needle (ε, s', 180−s'−ε); the other ≈ the original triangle minus the destroyed angle. Claim: by induction on the sorted triple, Mulan drives the triangle into an arbitrarily thin needle (ε, b, 180−b−ε) with b bounded by the surviving small angle, in bounded steps regardless of Shan-Yu's discard (BOTH children are "thinner" in a lexicographic sense).
  3. **Inclusion phase 2 — Euclidean extraction** (θ = 180°/n): From a needle (ε, b, 180−b−ε), cut to the large angle ~180−b−ε. Because 180 = nθ, choose β = kθ so that the two P-angles are kθ and (n−k)θ (supplementary, summing to nθ = 180). The surviving child carries a multiple kθ (or (n−k)θ). Iterate the subtractive Euclidean algorithm: reduce the large angle by θ-multiples until θ is exposed. Finite because 180 = nθ gives a finite Euclidean chain of length ≤ n−1. Potential = the large angle (or the Euclidean step count); strictly decreases. The ε only affects the discarded needle angle, not the produced θ, because θ|180 lets the final α be set exactly to the θ-creating position (β = kθ exactly, independent of ε).
  4. **Boundary n = 2 (θ = 90°):** one-move supplementary fork (β = 90°), as in lattice-descent.
  5. **Exclusion — δ-blocks-Euclid** (θ ∤ 180°): Write 180 = qθ + δ with δ ∈ (0, θ) (nonzero iff θ ∤ 180°). The supplementary pair (β, 180−β) can never be simultaneously θ-multiples terminating at θ: the subtractive Euclidean reduction on the large angle always leaves the nonzero remainder δ that cannot be eliminated. Shan-Yu maintains a thin-needle family whose large angle ≡ 180 (mod θ) ≡ δ ≠ 0 mod θ, so no cut exposes θ in the surviving child. For irrational θ, δ is never 0 (180/θ ∉ ℕ) and the Euclidean algorithm on (large angle, 180) never reaches θ.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - **Needle attraction terminates** — because each peeling cut makes both children lexicographically thinner (smallest angle strictly smaller), giving a natural-valued rank (e.g. number of small-angle peels remaining) that decreases. [THE UNPROVED CRUX OF THIS ROUTE.]
  - **Euclidean extraction lands exactly θ iff θ | 180** — because the supplementary P-pair (kθ, (n−k)θ) exists iff nθ = 180; otherwise the pair (β, 180−β) straddling θ-multiples has a gap δ = 180 mod θ that no integer k closes.
  - **ε does not contaminate the produced θ** — because θ|180 makes the θ-creating β = kθ independent of ε (ε lives only in the discarded needle angle).
Open gaps (builder fills):
  - **Phase-1 termination against adversarial Shan-Yu** (biggest gap): prove a strictly decreasing natural potential for the needle phase; the explorer's trace shows convergence but the prose proof must define the rank and show BOTH children decrease it (else Shan-Yu picks the non-decreasing one). The ε > 0 reality (P ≠ vertex, so α > 0) must be handled.
  - **Phase-2 precise Euclidean script:** which multiple k at each step, positivity of all child angles, and the exact finite bound (≤ n−1).
  - **Exclusion safe set** (biggest gap, likely break point): the compute explorer explicitly warned that "all thin needles" is NOT a closed safe set (e.g. (1, 35, 144) is NOT safe for θ = 72° — Mulan wins from it in 1 step). So "thin-needle family with large angle ≡ δ" is insufficient. The builder must either (a) strengthen to the full taint-free invariant (collapsing into lattice-descent's casework — acceptable as a fallback but note the framing convergence), or (b) find the true closed safe set for the needle framing. Flag this as the approach's likely failure mode.
  - Irrational θ and θ = 180·p/q (p > 1) in the Euclidean remainder framing.
Cases to cover: n = 2 boundary; rational non-divisor θ (δ rational nonzero); irrational θ (Euclidean algorithm on irrationals — the subtractive algorithm 180 − qθ leaves irrational δ, never reaches θ).
Watch out for: the needle-phase potential is the load-bearing unproved step; do NOT hand-wave "converges to needle" without a natural-valued decreasing rank — rigor rules require an explicit finite bound. The exclusion safe set is the likely fatal gap; if the builder cannot close it, this approach degrades to lattice-descent for the exclusion half.

### residue-monovariant: new
Target: Prove Mulan wins ⟺ θ = 180°/n. A single direct potential on θ-residues that Mulan strictly decreases regardless of Shan-Yu's discard, landing at 0 (all angles θ-multiples) iff θ | 180; then descend via (M2). Exclusion via the residue-sum obstruction (≡ δ ≠ 0) strengthened to a closed safe set.
Technique: Invariant/monovariant — a real-valued-then-naturalized residue potential + Euclidean descent on residues. The most "direct" framing: bypass the explicit lattice/needle detour, prove ONE potential suffices.
Skeleton:
  1. Cut operation. Define r(x) = x mod θ ∈ [0, θ).
  2. **Residue-sum identity:** r(A) + r(B) + r(C) ≡ A + B + C = 180° (mod θ). So if θ | 180°, the residue-sum ≡ 0; if θ ∤ 180°, the residue-sum ≡ δ := 180 mod θ ∈ (0, θ) ≠ 0. (Sum of three residues lies in [0, 3θ), so it equals one of {0, θ, 2θ} for divisor θ, or {δ, δ+θ, δ+2θ} for non-divisor.)
  3. **Inclusion — residue monovariant** (θ = 180°/n): Define Φ(A, B, C) = max(r(A), r(B), r(C)) (or, equivalently for termination, the triple of residues sorted descending — a lexicographic measure in a finite box). Claim: Mulan can always choose a cut (target vertex + α) so that BOTH children's Φ is strictly less than the parent's Φ, regardless of Shan-Yu's discard. Once Φ = 0, all residues are 0 → the triangle is on L_n → descend by (M2) (reduce move, level m → 1). Finite because Φ takes values in a finite set of residue-configurations (when θ = 180/n, residues are multiples of θ/n... — to be pinned down).
  4. **Boundary n = 2 (θ = 90°):** direct supplementary fork.
  5. **Exclusion — residue obstruction + closed safe set** (θ ∤ 180°): The residue-sum ≡ δ ≠ 0 forbids all three residues being 0 (so the lattice is unreachable). But this alone does NOT forbid a single angle = θ (residue 0 with the other two summing to δ). So strengthen: Shan-Yu maintains invariant I = "no angle ≡ 0 (mod θ)" (i.e. no multiple of θ), preserved by the 2×2 casework (same arithmetic as lattice-descent: both children tainted ⇒ parent tainted or θ = 180/n). Initial triangle: avoid the finite forbidden set {kθ}.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - **Residue-sum identity** — because r(x)+r(y)+r(z) ≡ x+y+z = 180 (mod θ), and residues are in [0, θ).
  - **Φ strictly decreases under Mulan's best move regardless of discard** — [THE UNPROVED CRUX]. Candidate mechanism: the supplementary P-pair (β, 180−β) has residues r(β) and r(180−β) = (−r(β)) mod θ; Mulan picks β so that both children's max-residue drops. The supplementary pairing gives Mulan control over TWO residues at once.
  - **Exclusion closure** — the 2×2 casework (borrowed from lattice-descent): both children tainted ⇒ parent tainted or θ = 180/n.
Open gaps (builder fills):
  - **Prove Φ strictly decreases for both children** (the crux): the invariant explorer flagged this as "conjectural in detail." Must show: for any taint-free triangle (Φ > 0), there is a cut such that whichever child Shan-Yu keeps has strictly smaller Φ. This is NONTRIVIAL — the supplementary pairing only directly controls two of the three residues per child, and the preserved third angle's residue is fixed. May require choosing the target vertex carefully (the one whose residue is largest). If this lemma fails, this approach's inclusion collapses (and the fallback is the lattice alignment move — i.e. convergence to lattice-descent).
  - **Naturalize Φ for the finite bound:** the residue box is continuous, so "strictly decreases" needs a natural-valued co-rank (e.g. the finite lattice-point count under Φ, or the level of the largest residue in units of θ/n). The builder must produce an explicit natural-valued decreasing potential, not just a real-valued one.
  - **Exclusion sufficiency:** the residue-sum obstruction is only necessary; the airtight exclusion is the 2×2 taint casework (shared with lattice-descent). State this honestly.
  - Irrational θ: residues of irrational multiples never sum to 0 mod θ unless exactly 0; the casework's 4th case has no solution.
Cases to cover: θ = 180/n (Φ-reduction then M2 descent); θ irrational; θ = 180·p/q (p > 1); θ > 90°.
Watch out for: the "Φ decreases for both children" lemma is the single most likely fatal gap across the whole field — if Mulan cannot force both children to improve, Shan-Yu picks the non-improving one and the monovariant fails. The fallback (lattice alignment move) rescues the inclusion but collapses this approach into lattice-descent. The builder should either prove the lemma or convert to the alignment move early rather than late.

### equilateral-witness: new
Target: Prove Mulan wins ⟺ θ = 180°/n. Exclusion-centric: for every non-divisor θ, exhibit the EQUILATERAL triangle (60°, 60°, 60°) as a single universal Shan-Yu start, closed under the supplementary reflection β ↔ 180−β. Inclusion via the supplementary-climb chain on the orbit {θ, 2θ, …, nθ = 180}.
Technique: Invariants — a single witness + supplementary-reflection closure (dual of crux aimo-0262: "self-reproducing invariant family restored after each opponent move"). Genuinely different exclusion framing: pin ONE triangle (equilateral) and prove it is a Shan-Yu win for EVERY θ ∤ 180°, via the reflection bouncing Mulan's θ-creating cuts back toward equilateral-ish survivors.
Skeleton:
  1. Cut operation; supplementary P-pair (β, 180−β).
  2. **Inclusion — supplementary-climb chain** (θ = 180°/n): The orbit O = {θ, 2θ, …, (n−1)θ} is closed under the supplementary involution kθ ↔ (n−k)θ. Mulan's alignment cut (β = kθ) creates the complementary pair (kθ, (n−k)θ) in the two children's P-slots. Whichever Shan-Yu keeps carries a multiple; descend by (M2) (level m → 1). Existence of the alignment cut: cut to the largest angle A; pigeonhole gives k with B < kθ < A+B (interval length A > θ). Bound ≤ n−1. (Shared arithmetic with lattice-descent, framed as orbit-climbing rather than lattice-membership.)
  3. **Boundary n = 2 (θ = 90°):** supplementary fork.
  4. **Exclusion — equilateral is the universal witness** (θ ∤ 180°): Claim: for every θ ∈ (0°, 180°) with θ ≠ 180°/n, the equilateral triangle E = (60°, 60°, 60°) is a Shan-Yu win. Prove by exhibiting a closed safe set S ⊇ {E} with: (i) no angle of any triangle in S equals θ; (ii) for every Mulan cut from a triangle in S, at least one child is in S. Template = the θ = 120° explicit proof: from E, cut to a vertex, β ∈ (60°, 120°); a 120°-angle in a child requires β ∈ {180°, 60°, 0°, 120°} (excluded), so no cut produces 120° in either child — E is closed. Generalize: for θ ∤ 180°, the supplementary reflection β ↔ 180−β maps any θ-targeting β to a value that keeps a child θ-free. The 2×2 casework (both children θ-bearing or both tainted) reduces to (k₁+k₂)θ = 180°, impossible for θ ∤ 180°.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - **Equilateral ∈ S for every non-divisor θ** — because the supplementary reflection β ↔ 180−β bounces θ-creating cuts back: setting a P-angle to θ forces the sibling's P-angle to 180−θ, and combined with the preserved 60° yields a survivor still in S. [The general closure is the unproved crux.]
  - **No single cut from E produces θ in either child when θ ∤ 180°** — because the four ways to place θ in a child's α-slot or P-slot each require β ∈ {θ, 180−θ, 60°+θ, 60°−θ, ...} and the supplementary partner lands outside θ; the only way BOTH children carry a θ-multiple is (k₁+k₂)θ = 180°.
  - **S is closed under the supplementary reflection** — the reflection generates a self-reproducing family (crux aimo-0262 template).
Open gaps (builder fills):
  - **Generalize the θ = 120° template to EVERY non-divisor θ** — this is the crux. The θ = 120° proof is explicit and clean, but for arbitrary θ (e.g. θ = 50°, θ = 72°, irrational θ) the builder must either (a) prove E itself is closed (no cut from E produces θ in either child — the 2×2 casework restricted to parent = E), or (b) exhibit a closed S ⊇ {E} defined by residue/interval conditions and prove closure under all β. The compute explorer noted (1, 35, 144) is NOT safe for θ = 72°, so S is not simply "all triangles" — it must be a genuine restricted set.
  - **E-only closure may be FALSE** for some θ (Mulan might cut E to a triangle from which she can force θ, even if not in one move). If E alone is not closed, the builder must find the smallest closed S containing E. This is the likely break point.
  - Inclusion arithmetic (supplementary-climb) is the same as lattice-descent's M1+M2 — the distinct contribution of this slug is the EXCLUSION. If the exclusion cannot be pinned on E, this approach degrades.
  - Irrational θ: E is closed iff no integer k₁+k₂ makes (k₁+k₂)θ = 180° (immediate for irrational θ).
Cases to cover: each non-divisor θ family — θ > 90° (E trivially closed: no θ = 180/n exceeds 90, and a single cut from E has all angles in (0, 120)∪{60}, so θ > 120 unreachable, 90 < θ < 120 needs the reflection); θ = 180·p/q (p > 1, e.g. θ = 72° = 180·2/5); irrational θ.
Watch out for: E-alone-closed might be FALSE — the builder must check whether Mulan can force θ from E in TWO+ moves for some non-divisor θ (the lattice sim says E ∈ S for tested non-divisors {54, 72, 100, 108, 120, 135, 144, 150}, but the real-game proof must show closure under ALL cuts, not just lattice cuts). The fallback is the full taint-free invariant (lattice-descent's casework), which rescues the exclusion but collapses this approach's distinctness.
