## imo-2026-02
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Concrete four-circle web, with a sharp midpoint conjecture.** Define
     \[
     D=BK\cap AC,\qquad E=CL\cap AB.
     \]
     The three given angle equalities become the explicit cyclicities
     \[
     BCDE,\qquad BDLN,\qquad CEKM.
     \]
     The first circle gives the power relation
     \(AD\cdot AC=AE\cdot AB\). Since \(M,N\) are the half-points of \(AB,AC\), this also forces \(D,E,M,N\) cyclic (equivalently, the crossed triangles \(ADE\) and \(ABC\) have the relevant SAS similarity). This produces a real Miquel/spiral-similarity network, not merely a suggestion. Let \(P\ne A\) and \(Q\ne A\) be the intersections of \((AKL)\) with \(AB\) and \(AC\). Numerical probing in several admissible configurations strongly suggests the much sharper statement
     \[
     P=\operatorname{mid}(B,E),\qquad Q=\operatorname{mid}(C,D).
     \]
     If this midpoint statement is established synthetically from the four circles, the whole target collapses immediately to the already proved product \(AB\cdot AE=AC\cdot AD\), because the powers at \(M,N\) become the corresponding quarter-products. **Exact hard step:** prove that \(A,K,L\) and the two stated midpoints are concyclic. Likely mechanism: a spiral-similarity/Miquel theorem applied to the cyclic quadruples \((BCDE),(BDLN),(CEKM),(DEMN)\). This is the strongest new opening found.
  2. **Inversion centered at \(A\), based on an actual invariant circle.** Set \(\rho^2=AB\cdot AE=AC\cdot AD\). Inversion of radius \(\rho\) centered at \(A\) interchanges \(B\leftrightarrow E\) and \(C\leftrightarrow D\), hence preserves the circle \((BCDE)\). Under it, \((BDLN)\) and \((CEKM)\) become circles through the exchanged base pairs \((E,C)\) and \((D,B)\). The circle \((AKL)\) becomes the line joining the inverse images of \(K,L\). The whole problem can therefore be reframed as a line/intersection statement for this inversion-paired four-circle configuration. **Exact hard step:** identify the image-line of \((AKL)\) (or a companion radical axis) in terms of the midpoint pairs \((B,E)\), \((C,D)\); inversion alone does not send \(K\) to \(L\), as a numerical check confirms, so do not assert that false shortcut.
  3. **Projective/harmonic midpoint formulation.** The conjectures \(P=\operatorname{mid}(B,E)\), \(Q=\operatorname{mid}(C,D)\) are equivalent to the harmonic relations
     \[
     (B,E;P,\infty_{AB})=-1,\qquad (C,D;Q,\infty_{AC})=-1.
     \]
     The cyclic web gives projectivities between the two base lines via the pencils through \(K\) and \(L\). A viable projective route would identify the induced involution on \(AB\cup AC\) and show that it sends the two points at infinity to the corresponding midpoint partners. **Exact hard step:** write the actual composition of perspectivities generated by \((BDLN)\) and \((CEKM)\); without this explicit map, “projective involution” is only vocabulary and should not be registered as an approach.
  4. **Power/second-intersection route, now with the missing concrete objects supplied.** Keep \(P,Q\) as above, but replace the vague search for triangles through second intersections of \(MK,NL\) by the points \(D,E\) and the three certified-by-angle circles. The desired equality is \(MA\cdot MP=NA\cdot NQ\), and the experimentally discovered midpoint identities determine both factors directly. **Exact hard step:** again the five-point concyclicity \(A,K,L,\operatorname{mid}(BE),\operatorname{mid}(CD)\); this is a much more precise target than the old unspecified reciprocal similarities.
