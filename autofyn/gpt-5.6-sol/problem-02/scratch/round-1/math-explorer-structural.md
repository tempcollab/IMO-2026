## imo-2026-02
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Equal-power reformulation (strongest structural opening).** Let \(\omega=(AKL)\). Since \(O\) is its centre, the target \(OM=ON\) is exactly \(\operatorname{Pow}_{\omega}(M)=\operatorname{Pow}_{\omega}(N)\). This removes the circumcentre entirely and changes the endpoint into a product/radical-axis statement. The midpoint facts \(AM=AB/2\), \(AN=AC/2\), together with the three paired angle equalities, make this substantially more natural than computing \(O\). A useful version is to let the lines \(AB,AC\) meet \(\omega\) again at \(P,Q\): the target becomes the directed-product identity \(MA\cdot MP=NA\cdot NQ\). The hard step is expressing or comparing \(MP,NQ\) from the given angle data without introducing uncontrolled auxiliary angles.
  2. **Three-angle algebraization.** Put \(x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL\), \(y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC\), and \(z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK\). The inside conditions fix the ray order, giving \(\angle ABL=x+y\) and \(\angle ACK=x+z\). The midpoint triangles immediately yield
     \[
     BK=\frac{AB}{2}\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)},\quad MK=\frac{AB}{2}\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+z)},
     \]
     \[
     CL=\frac{AC}{2}\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)},\quad NL=\frac{AC}{2}\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+y)}.
     \]
     These are verified applications of the sine law and expose a compact three-parameter structure. One can encode \(K\) as the intersection of the ray from \(B\) at angle \(x\) to \(BA\) with the ray from \(C\) at angle \(x+z\) to \(CA\), and similarly \(L\) using \(x+y\) and \(x\). The remaining midpoint-angle equations become two scalar trigonometric constraints. The target can then be tested through the circle equation rather than by synthetic centre chasing. The hard step is finding a factorization of those two constraints that yields the equal-power identity; brute expansion is likely large.
  3. **Linear circle-equation / vector reformulation.** Set \(A=0\), write \(B=b,C=c\), and write the circle \((AKL)\) as \(|X|^2-q\cdot X=0\), where \(q=2O\) and \(q\cdot K=|K|^2,\ q\cdot L=|L|^2\). Then
     \[
     OM=ON\iff q\cdot(c-b)=\frac{|c|^2-|b|^2}{2}.
     \]
     Equivalently, evaluating the circle polynomial at \(b/2,c/2\) gives the equal-power opening above. This is a clean endpoint certificate: only one linear identity in \(q\) is needed, not the coordinates of \(O\). The promising algebraic question is whether the angle constraints imply an especially simple decomposition of \(c-b\) in the basis \(K,L\), accompanied by the matching quadratic identity. The hard step is controlling signs and ray order when converting angle equalities to dot/cross-product equations.
  4. **Second-intersection similarity search.** On \(MK\) and \(NL\), let the second intersections with \(\omega\) be \(X,Y\). The target is \(MK\cdot MX=NL\cdot NY\) in directed lengths. The equalities \(\angle LBK=\angle LNC\) and \(\angle LCK=\angle BMK\) already pair angles based at \((B,N)\) and \((C,M)\), so a similarity involving \(B,C,M,N,X,Y,K,L\) is plausible. This is structurally different from solving the trigonometric system, though it has the same power endpoint. Stop at the search criterion: no such similarity has yet been established.
