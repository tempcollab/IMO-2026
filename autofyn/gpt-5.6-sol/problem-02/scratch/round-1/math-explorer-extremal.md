## imo-2026-02
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Circle-power invariant (strongest non-algebraic opening).** Let \(\omega=(AKL)\), and let its second intersections with \(AB,AC\) be \(P,Q\). The requested equality is exactly equality of the powers of the fixed midpoints \(M,N\) with respect to \(\omega\): since \(O\) is the center of \(\omega\), \(OM^2-ON^2=\operatorname{Pow}_\omega(M)-\operatorname{Pow}_\omega(N)\). On the two sidelines this reduces the target to the directed-product identity \(MA\cdot MP=NA\cdot NQ\). Thus the entire configuration can be viewed as forcing one scalar circle invariant rather than locating \(O\). This is closely aligned with the midpoint data and is the most promising route for this lens.
  2. **One-parameter deformation / conserved quantity.** Put \(\alpha=\angle KBA=\angle ACL\), \(\beta=\angle LBK=\angle LNC\), and \(\gamma=\angle LCK=\angle BMK\). For fixed \(ABC\), admissible configurations appear to form a one-parameter family indexed by \(\alpha\), with \(\beta,\gamma\) determined by the two midpoint conditions. Numerical evidence says the power difference \(D(\alpha)=\operatorname{Pow}_{(AKL)}(M)-\operatorname{Pow}_{(AKL)}(N)\) is identically zero; in the degenerate limit \(\alpha\to0^+\), the second intersections tend to \(P=M,Q=N\), so \(D\to0\). A synthetic deformation invariant would be genuinely far from a coordinate/structural-algebra proof. Hard point: justify existence/uniqueness and conservation without turning this into an opaque derivative computation.
  3. **Midpoint doubling as a finite geometric symmetry.** Dilate by factor \(2\) at \(B\) (sending \(M\) to \(A\)) and at \(C\) (sending \(N\) to \(A\)). The angle conditions at \(M,N\) then become angle conditions on the images of \(K,L\), suggesting a pairing of two doubled configurations whose circle powers at \(M,N\) match. This is the discrete/involutive analogue of the deformation opening. Hard point: identify a natural paired point or spiral similarity; no such involution has yet been verified.
  4. **Equal-power locus rather than circumcenter locus.** Because equal powers to a fixed circle define a tautological metric comparison with its center, one can target a radical-axis style statement for \(M,N\) after introducing an auxiliary circle through the midpoint-dilated images. This may permit a short cyclicity/similarity argument. No compelling auxiliary circle has yet emerged, so this is lower confidence than opening 1.

No parity/pigeonhole phenomenon is relevant in this geometry problem.

- Small-case / intuition notes: With \(A,B,C\) denoting the angles of \(ABC\), the local angle bookkeeping is consistent with
  \[
  BK=\frac{AB}{2}\frac{\sin\gamma}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}
     =BC\frac{\sin(C-\alpha-\gamma)}{\sin(A+2\alpha+\gamma)},
  \]
  and
  \[
  CL=\frac{AC}{2}\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}
     =BC\frac{\sin(B-\alpha-\beta)}{\sin(A+2\alpha+\beta)}.
  \]
  These are useful sanity constraints on any synthetic claim, not a proposed proof route. Numerical solutions for sample triangles \((A,B,C)=(70^\circ,60^\circ,50^\circ),(80^\circ,45^\circ,55^\circ),(50^\circ,80^\circ,50^\circ)\) and several admissible \(\alpha\) values gave equal midpoint powers to numerical precision (typically below \(10^{-14}\)). For example, for \((70^\circ,60^\circ,50^\circ)\), \(\alpha=8^\circ\), the normalized second intersections were \(AP/AB\approx0.5804262\), \(AQ/AC\approx0.5629280\), and both directed powers were approximately \(-0.0235980\) under the normalization \(BC=\sin A\). **Conjectural intuition:** as \(\alpha\to0^+\), \(P\to M\) and \(Q\to N\), supporting a conserved-zero power difference. These computations are evidence only, not proof.
