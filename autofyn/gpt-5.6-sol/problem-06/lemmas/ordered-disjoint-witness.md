# Ordered disjoint-witness lemma

Use the notation of the exact-support self-duality lemma. For every nonempty finite \(H\notin\mathcal U\), let \(\mu(H)\) be the least integer \(m\ge a_1\) with \(\operatorname{supp}(m)=H\). Then there is a term \(a_j<\mu(H)\) such that \(\operatorname{supp}(a_j)\cap H=\varnothing\). Consequently \(\operatorname{rad}(a_j)\le a_j<\mu(H)\).

The minimum exists because powers of \(\prod_{p\in H}p\) have exact support \(H\) and are arbitrarily large. Since \(H\notin\mathcal U\), the static gcd-polar lemma says that \(\mu(H)\) is not a term. (In particular, it cannot equal \(a_1\).) Choose \(i\) with \(a_i<\mu(H)<a_{i+1}\). Because \(a_{i+1}\) is the least stage-\(i\) admissible integer above \(a_i\), the skipped integer \(\mu(H)\) is coprime to some \(a_j\), \(j\le i\). Hence the supports are disjoint and \(a_j\le a_i<\mu(H)\). The radical inequality is immediate.
