## imo-2026-06
- Distinct openings: **(1) Prime-rooted radical descent on inclusion-minimal positive supports.** Let \(M\) be an inclusion-minimal member of \(\mathcal U\) and fix \(p\in M\). The negative set \(M\setminus\{p\}\) has an ordered witness \(K\in\mathcal U\) disjoint from it. Linkedness forces \(p\in K\); taking an inclusion-minimal positive subset \(N\subseteq K\) preserves \(p\). Whenever \(\operatorname{rad}(M\setminus\{p\})\ge a_1\), the identity \(\mu(M\setminus\{p\})=\operatorname{rad}(M\setminus\{p\})\) makes \(\operatorname{rad}(N)<\operatorname{rad}(M)\). This is the strongest new opening: iterate while retaining the chosen prime \(p\), and stop when the cofactor support has radical below \(a_1\). **(2) Finite terminal-state classification.** At a terminal minimal support \(N\ni p\), put \(S=N\setminus\{p\}\). If \(S\ne\varnothing\), then \(\operatorname{rad}(S)<a_1\), so only finitely many such \(S\) exist; applying the ordered witness once more gives \(p<\mu(S)\), a bound depending only on one of those finitely many \(S\). If \(S=\varnothing\), the positive singleton \(\{p\}\) must meet \(\operatorname{supp}(a_1)\), so \(p\mid a_1\). Thus the terminal states suggest a uniform finite bound on every prime occurring in every inclusion-minimal member of \(\mathcal U\). **(3) Infinite-new-prime contradiction formulation.** Rather than growing anchors, assume minimal positive supports use unbounded primes, root the preceding descent at one such prime, and force it into the finite terminal list. This reaches finite control without any inference from a selected witness being disjoint from a later enlarged trace. Once minimal positive supports use only bounded primes, their union is finite and every positive finite support contains one of them; this is the direct bridge to the already certified finite-control-to-periodicity reduction. These are related formulations of the bounded-radical route, not completed proofs.
- Candidate technique(s): Inclusion-minimal members of an upward-closed family; a monovariant given by the integer radical; descent that preserves a marked prime; threshold split \(\operatorname{rad}(S)\ge a_1\) versus \(<a_1\); finite exhaustion of squarefree supports below \(a_1\). The key quantitative fact is not merely \(\operatorname{rad}(a_j)<\mu(H)\), but that for \(H=M\setminus\{p\}\) the witness must contain the deleted prime \(p\), while \(\mu(H)=\operatorname{rad}(H)\) above the threshold.
- Cheap-kill candidates: First replace arbitrary positive supports by inclusion-minimal ones (finite deletion). Fix one prime throughout the descent; do not allow the marked prime to change. Split off singleton minimal supports immediately. Below \(a_1\), use that a squarefree radical determines a support and there are only finitely many possibilities. No CRT or global enumeration is needed until the finite prime bound is obtained.
