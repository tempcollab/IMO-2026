## imo-2026-06
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Compact-space extension, mainly as a diagnostic rather than an automatic solution.** Put the prime set in the Cantor cube \(2^{\mathbb P}\), and let \(\mathcal C=\{X\subseteq\mathbb P:X\cap K\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }K\in\mathcal U\}\). Each condition “\(X\cap K\ne\varnothing\)” is clopen because \(K\) is finite, so \(\mathcal C\) is compact closed and its finite points are exactly \(\mathcal U\). This makes the desired finite-prime control precisely a clopen/cylinder conclusion for the finite points. Compactness alone does **not** give that conclusion: an arbitrary intersection of clopen conditions is merely closed, and extracting finitely many conditions requires an already-proved open cover (or openness of the relevant complement). Likewise, compactness applied to each individual finite \(H\) only gives finite information depending on \(H\), not one uniform prime set. Thus any compactness route must establish an additional openness/isolation statement from the greedy order; it cannot cite compactness by itself.
  2. **Well-founded finite-character recursion by the numerical rank \(\mu\).** The certified ordered-witness property strengthens self-duality to the exact equivalence, for every nonempty finite \(H\),
     \[H\notin\mathcal U\iff \exists K\in\mathcal U\ (K\cap H=\varnothing\text{ and }\mu(K)<\mu(H)).\]
     The reverse implication is self-duality, and the forward implication uses the earlier term witness plus \(\mu(K)\le a_j<\mu(H)\). For each fixed \(H\), there are only finitely many supports \(K\) with \(\mu(K)<\mu(H)\), since they are supports of integers below \(\mu(H)\). Hence membership has genuine finite character at every rank. The unresolved issue is uniformity: the finite predecessor set grows with \(\mu(H)\). A viable whole-problem framing would therefore target stabilization of the *minimal positive supports* in this well-founded recursion, rather than assume a finite controller at the outset.
  3. **Minimal-positive-edge / self-blocking clutter framing.** Let \(\mathcal M\) be the inclusion-minimal members of \(\mathcal U\). Finite prime control is equivalent to proving that \(\bigcup\mathcal M\) is finite (indeed, that \(\mathcal M\) is a finite self-blocking clutter). If \(H\in\mathcal M\), then for every \(p\in H\), the proper subset \(H\setminus\{p\}\) is negative, so the ordered witness gives an earlier positive support \(K_p\) with \(K_p\cap H=\{p\}\). This is a rigid “private crossing witness” condition absent from a generic maximal linked family. It points to extremal set-system, sunflower, or bounded-transversal arguments, but no standard WQO applies directly: finite subsets under inclusion have the singleton antichain.
  4. **Replace topology by a small-prime descent.** Computational evidence and the close corpus analogue `aimo-0030` suggest targeting the stronger assertion that every two positive supports meet in a prime at most \(a_1\), or in some bounded prime set generated during a descent. This would immediately make all larger primes inessential. The natural obstruction is a pair of terms whose common primes are all large; choosing a numerically minimal such pair may allow replacement by an exact-support number below it, just as `aimo-0030` turns a large-prime-only intersection into a smaller violating pair. This is genuinely different from compactness because it seeks an explicit finite bound rather than a finite-subcover theorem.
- Candidate technique(s): Cantor-cube compactness only after proving openness; well-founded induction on \(\mu(H)\); minimal transversals/self-blocking clutters; extremal principle and minimal-counterexample descent; possible sunflower/bounded-transversal pruning. The key warning is that pointwise finite character is not uniform finite control.
- Cheap-kill candidates: Fix one positive edge \(E=\operatorname{supp}(a_1)\), so every positive support meets the finite set \(E\). Work only with inclusion-minimal positive supports. For each element of such a support, use the earlier private crossing witness \(K_p\cap H=\{p\}\). Before invoking topology, test whether these witnesses force a size bound or a bounded prime via pigeonhole over their traces on \(E\). Also test a minimal counterexample pair sharing only primes \(>a_1\), as in `aimo-0030`.

- Analogous past problems (cruxes):
  - `aimo-0030` — genuinely close. In a gcd-defined family of “good” numbers, a minimal-counterexample descent upgrades pairwise sharing of some prime to sharing a bounded (“small”) prime; it also normalizes a number by deleting large prime factors while keeping its small-prime signature and controlling its size. This is the best analogue for replacing nonuniform compactness with explicit finite prime control.
  - `aimo-0678` — analogous at the stabilization level. A minimum-of-a-set integer monovariant is nonincreasing, hence stabilizes and bounds the state, after which periodicity follows from finiteness. Here the corresponding candidate is a rank/minimal-obstruction parameter for new essential supports, though no such monotone parameter has yet been identified.
  - `aimo-0477` — weaker but relevant: a divisor chain inside the finite divisor lattice of a fixed integer stabilizes, converting an infinite recurrence to finite state. It suggests that success requires placing traces/witnesses in a genuinely finite lattice (for example primes bounded by an explicit integer), not merely calling the full Cantor cube compact.

- Dead ends (do not retry): A bare “compactness gives finitely many primes/clauses” is invalid: closed subsets of \(2^{\mathbb P}\) need not be clopen, and compactness gives finite subcovers only from open covers. Finite subsets are not WQO under inclusion (singletons form an infinite antichain), so Dickson-style stabilization is unavailable without a different encoding. The round-1 one-stage anchor set fails: chosen witnesses disjoint from a trace \(A=H\cap E\) need not be disjoint from all of \(H\cap P\), and pairwise linkedness does not imply the triple-intersection property needed for the positive direction. CRT avoidance without a compatible global assignment and first-disagreement without a terminating rank were already rejected.
