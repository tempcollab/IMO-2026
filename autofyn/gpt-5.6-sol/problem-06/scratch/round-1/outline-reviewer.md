For every \(A\subseteq E\) that is not in \(\mathcal U\), self-duality supplies \(B_A\in\mathcal U\) disjoint from \(A\). Put \(P=E\cup\bigcup_{A\subseteq E,\,A\notin\mathcal U}B_A\), a finite set. Then for every finite \(H\), membership of \(H\) is determined by \(H\cap P\): if \(H\in\mathcal U\), upward closure gives \(H\cap P\in\mathcal U\) once one proves it meets every positive set; if \(H\notin\mathcal U\), let \(A=H\cap E\), note \(A\notin\mathcal U\), and use \(B_A\subseteq P\), disjoint from \(H\), to see \(H\cap P\notin\mathcal U\). The builder should spell out the first implication via self-duality: any positive set meets \(E\) in a subset \(C\); if \(C\subseteq H\) it meets \(H\cap P\), while if not, the assertion requires a careful direct argument or an equivalent finite trace formulation. A bare “no infinite branch” compactness claim is not enough.
- There is an even simpler finite-trace formulation the builder should seek: classify each positive set by its trace on \(E\), and choose one finite disjoint witness for each negative trace. This is the credible route to closing the theorem and is why this approach survives despite the open lemma.
- Step 6 must define the admitted residues and handle the boundary at \(a_1\). Once membership is genuinely invariant under adding \(L\) for all positive integers, translation by \(L\) maps the admitted set intersected with \([a_1,\infty)\) bijectively to its portion intersected with \([a_1+L,\infty)\); exactly \(T\) admitted integers lie in \([a_1,a_1+L)\), yielding the indexed identity for every \(n\).
