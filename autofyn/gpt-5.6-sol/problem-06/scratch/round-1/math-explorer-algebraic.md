## imo-2026-06
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Periodic-sieve / Boolean-clause framing (recommended algebraic route).** For an integer `m`, write `U(m)={x: gcd(x,m)>1}=⋃_{p|m}pℤ`. Before choosing `a_{n+1}`, the admissible integers form `A_n=⋂_{i≤n}U(a_i)`, an intersection of periodic divisibility sieves, and `a_{n+1}` is the next integer of `A_n`. Thus every finite stage is periodic modulo `rad(lcm(a_1,…,a_n))`. The whole claim would follow from an eventual finite-control statement: beyond some height, the infinite intersection is governed by finitely many prime-divisibility clauses (or, more weakly, agrees on a tail with a fixed periodic set). Then the sequence is simply the increasing enumeration of a periodic set, which gives `a_{n+T}=a_n+L`. This is genuinely different from a gap/increment induction: its top-level target is stabilization of a descending family of periodic sets.
  2. **Prime-support hypergraph / transversal framing.** Replace each term by the finite set `S_i={p:p|a_i}`. The condition is that the new support is a transversal of all earlier `S_i`, while the chosen integer is the least larger integer realizing such a transversal. All `S_i` meet the fixed finite set `P=S_1`. Split candidates by their nonempty `P`-support `Q⊆P`. For a fixed `Q`, only earlier supports disjoint from `Q` inside `P` impose further outside-prime requirements. There are finitely many `Q`; if each corresponding outside-prime transversal condition either becomes impossible below the moving height or acquires a finite kernel, the surviving candidate types are finite unions of CRT residue classes. This isolates a possible finite-combinatorial core rather than tracking every gcd separately.
  3. **Normalized phase / translation dynamics.** A universal bound is immediate: `a_n+rad(a_1)` is admissible, since every earlier term shares some prime divisor of `a_1`, and `rad(a_1)` is divisible by all of them. Hence every increment is at most `R=rad(a_1)`. Study the translated local configuration near `a_n` modulo a candidate modulus `M`; if all active old constraints can be represented by finitely many residue masks, the bounded-gap greedy map becomes a deterministic map on a finite state space. A repeated state gives a translated cycle, directly matching the requested relation rather than ordinary eventual periodicity of values. The key missing ingredient is a finite-memory theorem for old constraints.
- Candidate technique(s): CRT representation of finite divisibility sieves; squarefree/radical reduction; finite-state deterministic dynamics modulo a fixed modulus; monotone Boolean CNF/hitting-set language for prime supports; possible Noetherian/compactness-style finite-kernel statement, but only if made effective on a numerical tail.
- Cheap-kill candidates: Set `R=rad(a_1)`. Since every previous `a_i` has `gcd(a_i,a_1)>1`, the candidate `a_n+R` has gcd greater than 1 with every `a_i`; therefore `1≤a_{n+1}-a_n≤R`. Also exponents of primes are irrelevant to admissibility, so replace every term by its radical in all gcd tests. These two reductions should precede any heavy finite-state construction.

Caution against the naive assertion that the finite-stage modulus `rad(lcm(a_1,…,a_n))` stabilizes: later terms can introduce arbitrarily large new prime factors, and periodicity at each finite stage alone does not yield one eventual modulus. Likewise, pairwise intersection of finite prime supports does not by itself imply a finite common prime or a finite transversal (families such as `{p,q_i}` show why); any hypergraph argument must exploit greedy order and the bounded numerical gaps. A generic “bounded increments imply periodic increments” claim is false without finite memory.
