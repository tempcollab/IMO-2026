## imo-2026-06
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Bounded-gap / finite-state framing.** There is an immediate uniform gap bound: the next multiple of $a_1$ after $a_n$ is admissible, since every earlier $a_i$ has $\gcd(a_i,a_1)>1$; hence $1\le a_{n+1}-a_n\le a_1$. Also any prime shared by consecutive terms divides their difference, so every $a_n$ for $n\ge2$ has a prime divisor at most $a_1$. This points to encoding each local move by residues modulo a modulus supported on the finitely many primes $\le a_1$, then seeking recurrence of a translated state. The genuine obstacle is proving that old constraints witnessed only by primes $>a_1$ eventually carry no extra information; bounded gaps alone does not justify truncating the history.
  2. **Translated-block extremal framing.** Partition the line into blocks of length $a_1$ (or experimentally $2a_1$ for many odd squarefree starts) and compare the greedy admissible set in two translated blocks. A first-difference/minimal-counterexample comparison could exploit that translation preserves all gcd witnesses coming from primes dividing the translation. The target is not periodicity of numerical values in a bounded state, but stabilization of the ordered pattern of occupied residues in consecutive blocks. Obstacle: a gcd witness may be a prime not dividing the proposed translation, so direct translation invariance fails until one proves a finite “core-prime” phenomenon.
  3. **Prime-support intersection framing.** Replace each term by its finite set of prime divisors; the sequence is a clique in the intersection graph of prime supports, and the greedy rule chooses the least next integer whose support meets every previous support. Consecutive supports always intersect in a prime $\le a_1$, giving a finite alphabet for adjacency. An extremal candidate is a minimal finite subfamily of earlier supports whose transversal condition already determines admissibility; if such certificates can be bounded uniformly, residues modulo the product of their primes give a deterministic finite automaton up to translation. This framing differs from a likely direct recurrence/CRT rival because it attacks the unbounded memory in the definition via finite certificates rather than guessing a period.
  4. **Gap-word framing.** The gap word $d_n=a_{n+1}-a_n$ lies in the finite alphabet $\{1,\dots,a_1\}$. The claim is exactly eventual periodicity of this word (with $L$ the sum over one period). A structural route would seek a monotone “constraint profile” attached to each gap symbol or each residue window, rather than manipulate the $a_n$ directly. The obstacle is that a finite alphabet does not itself imply periodicity: one must show the greedy future is determined by a bounded suffix or another finite profile.
- Candidate technique(s): Extremal principle on the first block/state that fails to repeat; invariant/monovariant measuring the set of still-essential prime witnesses; finite-state reduction modulo an eventual core modulus; prime-support set-system encoding; bounded gaps followed by pigeonhole only after determinism of the reduced state is established.
- Cheap-kill candidates: The uniform bound $a_{n+1}-a_n\le a_1$ is the strongest cheap structural pruning. Consequently a consecutive common prime is at most $a_1$, and every term after the first has a prime factor at most $a_1$. Also test whether an eventual common prime appears: if some prime divides all terms from a point onward, the greedy sequence thereafter should collapse to successive multiples of that prime, giving $T=1$; computations show this handles many starts but not starts such as $15,105,385,1001$. No parity-only argument handles odd multi-prime starts.
- Knowledge-base entries to use: **Pigeonhole / extremal principle**; **Invariants & monovariants**; **Order of an element, Fermat/Euler / eventual periodicity modulo $m$** (only after a finite-state encoding is valid); **Divisor analysis and gcd structure**; **Reformulate** (integer process to prime-support intersection graph); **Solve a simpler / special case first**. The KB’s generic finite-state-periodicity idea is relevant, but it does not supply the missing proof that the infinite history compresses to finitely many states.

- Dead ends (do not retry): No recorded failed approaches. However, two tempting unsupported shortcuts should be avoided: (i) “bounded gaps imply eventual periodicity”; and (ii) reducing every term modulo a fixed modulus without first showing that gcd witnesses from arbitrarily large primes are redundant. Also false is the guess that the sequence simply enumerates integers having nontrivial gcd with $a_1$: for $a_1=15$, $21$ has gcd $3$ with $15$ but is skipped because it is coprime to the prior term $20$.
- Small-case / intuition notes: Computation (evidence only) gives constant-gap tails for prime powers and many even starts. For $a_1=15$, the eventual residue cycle modulo $30$ is $18,20,24,0,6,10,12,15$ (gap period $8$, translation $L=30$). For $a_1=105$, a detected gap period has length $58$ and translation $210$; for $a_1=1001$, length $282$ and translation $2002$. For $a_1=385$, no period of length at most $2000$ was detected in a 5000-term run, although tail residues show conspicuous block behavior modulo $770$; this is a warning that transients/periods can be long. Conjectural shape: for odd squarefree starts with several prime factors, translation by $2a_1$ is common, while prime-power starts often collapse to translation by their least prime divisor. This pattern should guide state/modulus experiments, not be asserted as a theorem.
- How this framing differs from likely rivals: This route does not try to identify $T,L$ algebraically from the initial factorization and does not begin with a direct CRT construction of the next term. It treats the sequence as a bounded-step greedy walk with unbounded memory and makes compression of that memory—the finite certificate/core-prime issue—the top-level structural target.
