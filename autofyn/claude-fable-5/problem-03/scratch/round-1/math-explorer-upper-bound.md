## imo-2026-03 (Xiang Yu's side — upper-bound / adversary structure)

### Setup reduction (verified, not just assumed)
Claiming sub-game: pieces sorted desc `L1≥L2≥...≥Lm`; first-mover greedy ("always take current largest") is optimal for BOTH players (standard exchange-argument fact for alternating-pick-to-maximize-own-sum games — easy induction: swapping any non-greedy move for the greedy one never hurts the mover and never helps the opponent). Consequence used throughout: Liu gets the odd-ranked pieces (`L1,L3,L5,...`), Xiang gets the even-ranked ones. If both players use all their marks, total marks = 2n (distinct), pieces = 2n+1, so Liu ends up with `n+1` pieces (odd ranks) and Xiang with `n` (even ranks).

**Structural trivial lower bound (always true, no strategy needed):** for any final sorted piece vector `a1≥a2≥...≥a_{2n+1}≥0`, pairing `(a1,a2),(a3,a4),...,(a_{2n-1},a_{2n})` gives `a_{2i-1}≥a_{2i}` termwise, plus the unpaired `a_{2n+1}≥0` tacked onto Liu's side. Hence **Liu's total ≥ 1/2 always**, for every configuration, regardless of either player's strategy. This is a free fact — cite it, don't reprove it from scratch each time. It immediately kills any conjecture `c(n)=1/2` unless Xiang can force the gap to vanish in the limit for every Liu split (this DOES happen for careless/symmetric Liu splits, see below), so the real question is how much *above* 1/2 Liu can force by choosing his split cleverly.

### Distinct openings I pursued
1. **n=1 full closed-form solve** (2 pieces from Liu, 1 Xiang cut → 3 final pieces, Liu gets rank1+rank3 = 1 − median). Solved exactly: with `x` = larger Liu-piece fraction (`x≥1/2`), Xiang's best achievable median is `M(x) = max(x/2, 1−x)`, verified against dense numerical brute force to 5+ significant digits (see script output: matches exactly at x=0.5,0.55,...,0.9). Liu minimizes `M(x)` at `x=2/3`, giving `M=1/3`, so **c(1) = 2/3**, attained by Liu splitting the stick 2:1 and Xiang's counter splitting the 2/3-piece exactly in half (giving three equal 1/3 pieces — Liu takes two of them).

2. **Numerical minimax search for n=2** (piece counts 3→5). Built a full brute-force-over-compositions + `scipy.differential_evolution` inner solver for Xiang's best response to any Liu 3-split, then searched Liu's split. Key finding: symmetric/naive Liu splits (equal thirds, or any split where two of the three pieces are equal, or where the largest piece exactly equals the sum of the other two) let Xiang drive Liu's total **down to exactly 1/2** via a "match-and-shave" trick (pair up two already-equal or exactly-matchable pieces, then shave/split the remainder into a matching pair, leaving one negligible piece as the sole odd-rank leftover). Liu must avoid all such coincidental matches. The true optimum is the **geometric split (4/7, 2/7, 1/7)** (ratio 4:2:1), verified via differential evolution over ALL 6 compositions of 2 cuts among 3 pieces (comps `(2,0,0),(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,2,0),(0,1,1),(0,0,2)`) to give value exactly `4/7 ≈ 0.571428...` — and perturbing this split in any direction (tested `(0.6,.28,.12), (0.55,.30,.15), (0.62,.26,.12), (0.58,.27,.15), (0.6,.3,.1)`) strictly *decreases* the guaranteed value to `~0.55–0.56`, confirming (4/7,2/7,1/7) is a strict local (and, given the pattern below, almost certainly global) maximum. **Conjecture: c(2) = 4/7.**

3. **n=3 spot-check.** Conjectured general formula (see below) predicts `c(3)=8/15`. Tested the geometric construction `(8,4,2,1)/15` against all `C(3+3,3)=20` compositions of 3 cuts among 4 pieces via differential evolution: numeric best = `0.53333332...` = `8/15` to 8 significant figures, with Xiang's optimal composition being `(3,0,0,0)` (all three cuts on the single largest piece `8/15`), matching the predicted mechanism exactly.

### The Xiang Yu reply structure that emerges (analyzed exactly, both algebraically and numerically)
At the extremal Liu split (geometric, ratio `2^n : 2^{n-1} : ... : 2 : 1`, i.e. `a_i = 2^{n+1-i}/(2^{n+1}-1)` for `i=1..n+1`), the identity `a1 = (a2+a3+...+a_{n+1}) + a_{n+1}` holds exactly (since `2^n = (2^{n-1}+...+2+1) + 1`). Xiang's optimal reply: **use all `n` cuts on the single largest piece `a1`, splitting it into exact duplicate copies of `a2, a3, ..., a_{n+1}` plus one extra copy of the smallest piece `a_{n+1}`.** This creates the final multiset `{a1-copy=a2, a2, a1-copy=a3, a3, ..., a1-copy=a_{n+1}, a_{n+1}, a_{n+1}(extra)}` — i.e. every value `a2,...,a_{n+1}` now appears in an exact duplicate pair (adjacent in sorted order), plus one extra `a_{n+1}` left over at the very bottom. Liu's alternating pick then gets exactly one copy from each duplicate pair (rank parity guarantees this) PLUS the leftover smallest piece at the tail (since 2n+1 is odd, the last rank is Liu's). Net result: Liu's total telescopes to exactly `a1/2 + a2/2 + a2/2 + ... ` — algebraically this collapses to the recursion:

`c(n) = a1/2 + c(n-1)·(1 − a1)`, self-consistently solved by `a1 = 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)` and `c(n) = 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)`, with the boundary condition `a1 = 2·a2` (i.e. Liu's optimal split sits exactly at the threshold where "split a1 in half" stops dominating and the recursive duplicate-trick takes over) — this threshold coincidence is why the same numeric value 4/7 (for n=2) appeared identically across THREE different Xiang compositions `(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(2,0,0)` in the differential-evolution run — a strong consistency signal, not a coincidence.

### Candidate closed-form answer (CONJECTURE, numerically confirmed n=1,2,3, not yet proved)
**c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} − 1).**
- n=1: 2/3 ✓ (verified analytically + numerically)
- n=2: 4/7 ✓ (verified numerically to high precision, robust to perturbation)
- n=3: 8/15 ✓ (verified numerically to 8 sig figs)
- Liu's extremal construction: mark cuts so the n+1 pieces have lengths `2^n, 2^{n-1}, ..., 2, 1` (all over `2^{n+1}-1`) — a **dyadic/geometric sequence where the largest piece exceeds the sum of all the rest by exactly the smallest piece**.

Inductive step: given ANY Liu split `a1≥...≥a_{n+1}` (not just the extremal one), show Xiang has a response forcing Liu ≤ `2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)`. The natural inductive strategy: compare `a1` to `1/2`. If `a1 ≥ 1/2`... (case-split not yet fully worked out — this is the actual gap the outliner/builder must close). The mechanism above (duplicate-and-shave using cuts concentrated on the largest piece, recursing on the rest) is the right shape but needs to be shown valid (i.e. rank-order preserving) for arbitrary, not just extremal, Liu configurations — likely via a monotonicity/exchange argument showing any deviation of Liu's split from the geometric one only helps Xiang.
- **Lower bound (Liu's side):** exhibit the explicit geometric-ratio construction and directly verify, by the same duplicate-argument computation, that it guarantees exactly `2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)` against EVERY Xiang response (this direction needs Xiang's move algebra worked out generally — for a fixed Liu split, characterizing Xiang's true optimum over all compositions/cut placements, generalizing the closed form `M(x)=max(x/2,1-x)` found for n=1).

### Cheap-kill candidates
- The **rank-pairing parity argument** (`a_{2i-1}≥a_{2i}` termwise) instantly gives Liu ≥ 1/2 with zero casework — use it to immediately dismiss any approach or conjectured answer with `c(n) < 1/2` or `c(n)` not `> 1/2` for finite n.
- **Symmetric/equal-piece Liu splits are always dominated** — any approach that has Liu split evenly (or with any two-piece coincidental equality) is a dead end: Xiang's shave-and-match trick drives it straight to 1/2, strictly worse than the geometric split. Reject "equal split" as a candidate construction outright.
